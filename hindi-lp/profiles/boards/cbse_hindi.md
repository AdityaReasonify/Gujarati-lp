## Grade-band language

### Class 3 — eight to nine

The bottom rung of the ladder. The band was set one step below class 5's *measured* band, and
**the first pilot chapter has now been measured against it** — ch8 चतुर गीदड़ (एकांकी, 11 topics):
`explanation` **39–49, median 44**; `real_life_example` **17–21, median 21**; verbatim chunk
**22–70, median 43**. The band held, so it stands.

It was deliberately not extrapolated from class 6: class 10 was calibrated against class 7,
skipped two rungs, and inverted, and that inversion is still on record as the cost. The first
emit of ch8 came out at a median of **56** — inside no band and only ten words below class 5's
66, which would have left no room for class 4 — and was trimmed to the band rather than the band
widened to it. **Measure the first chapter of every grade before authoring the second.**

| | class 3 (measured, 1 chapter) | class 5 (measured, 2 chapters) |
|---|---|---|
| `explanation` | **35–55 words**, median **44** | 55–75, medians 67 · 65 |
| `real_life_example` | **15–25 words** — one sentence, one thing the child has done; median **21** | 20–35, medians 30 · 25 |
| `real_life_example` reach | **only what this child has personally done** — घर, कक्षा, खेल, खाना, त्योहार | personal and concrete; never public or social |
| craft naming | **noticing only.** Point at the तुक, the लय, the दोहराव; do **not** say the word *तुक* until the child has heard the two words said together | noticing before naming |
| the closing question | "तुम्हें क्या अच्छा लगा?" / "तुम्हारे साथ क्या होता है?" — preference and memory only | memory and preference only |
| sentence length | **one clause per sentence, always** — not "wherever the meaning allows" | one clause wherever the meaning allows |

Four things are specific to this grade and none of them is "use easier words":

- **`figures_of_speech` is `[]` for very nearly every topic in this book, and that is correct.**
  Naming an अलंकार is out of range at eight. `reference/alankar_chhand.md` already makes an अलंकार
  named but not present a defect at every band; here the temptation is highest, because the poems
  look simple enough to decorate.
- **The chunk is very short.** Expect roughly **40–80 words** of verbatim per prose topic and one
  छंद per verse topic. वीणा class 3 carries only 6,518 printed words of main reading across 154
  pages — **42 words per page**.
- **The अभ्यास is most of the book, and it is done, not written.** Matching, colouring, guessing,
  singing, acting. Nine of eighteen chapters have their first अभ्यास banner on page 1, 2 or 3.
  `exercise_solutions.json` is the larger deliverable and is produced with the chapter.
- **शिक्षण-संकेत is addressed to the teacher** and belongs to neither deliverable — see
  §अभ्यास block names below.

### Class 4 — nine to ten

Between class 3 and class 5, and **provisional on the same terms**. Class 4's reading is materially
longer than class 3's — 52 words per printed page against 42, and ch12 शतरंज में मात alone runs
1,638 reading words — so the chunk grows before the language does.

| | class 3 | **class 4 (provisional)** | class 5 (measured) |
|---|---|---|---|
| `explanation` | 35–55 | **45–65 words**, target median ~55 | 55–75 |
| `real_life_example` | 15–25 | **18–30 words** | 20–35 |
| chunk per prose topic | 40–80 words | **50–100 words** | 60–120 words |
| craft naming | noticing only, never named | **name तुक and दोहराव once**, after the child has heard them | noticing before naming |
| the closing question | preference and memory | may ask **what the child would do** | memory and preference |

`figures_of_speech` stays `[]` for most topics; मानवीकरण is the one device this book genuinely
carries — ch1 चिड़िया का गीत narrates in a bird's own voice, ch9 मिठाइयों का सम्मेलन holds a
conference of sweets — and it is worth one sentence where the text plainly does it, nowhere else.

**Record the measured medians for both grades after the pilots** (`CLASS3_PLAN.md` §5 Phase B,
`CLASS4_PLAN.md` §5), so the ladder gains two rungs instead of two more uncalibrated ones.

### Class 5 — ten to eleven

The bottom rung. **These numbers are measured, not chosen** — from the two pilot chapters,
ch9 न्याय (एकांकी, 17 topics) and ch6 चतुर चित्रकार (mixed, 13 topics). That is the standard class 9
set and the one class 10 failed to meet, so it is applied here from the first chapter rather than
after the class.

| | class 5 | class 6 |
|---|---|---|
| `explanation` | **55–75 words** (medians 67 · 65; full span 56–74 over 30 topics) | 55–90 stated, **76 measured** |
| `real_life_example` | **20–35 words** (medians 30 · 25) — one anchor, one or two sentences | 72 measured |
| `real_life_example` reach | **entirely personal and concrete** — घर, कक्षा, खेल, त्योहार, खाना. Never a public or social situation | one everyday anchor, close to home |
| craft naming | **noticing, not naming a device** — "ये दो शब्द एक जैसे बजते हैं" before ever saying *तुक* | name the device, show it once |
| the closing question | "तुम्हारे साथ क्या होता है?" — memory and preference only | "क्या तुम्हारे साथ ऐसा हुआ है?" |
| sentence length | **one clause per sentence** wherever the meaning allows | short sentences |

What carries over unchanged from every band: Devanagari throughout, Roman only inside brackets on
first use of a technical term, second-person teacher voice, no lecturing, and no assumed prior
knowledge of the literary form.

Three things are specific to this grade and none of them is "use easier words":

- **`figures_of_speech` is `[]` for most topics, and that is correct.** मानवीकरण in
  *चाँद का कुरता* and *किरन* is real and worth one sentence; anything requiring the child to hold
  a comparison they cannot see is out of range. `reference/alankar_chhand.md` — an अलंकार named
  but not present is a defect at every band, and at this one the temptation is highest because the
  poems look simple enough to decorate.
- **The chunk is short.** Expect roughly 60–120 words of verbatim per prose topic and one
  छंद-इकाई per verse topic. वीणा's whole class carries only 8,449 printed words of main reading.
- **The अभ्यास is larger than the text.** In no chapter of वीणा is the main reading more than a
  third of the pages, so `exercise_solutions.json` is the *larger* deliverable and is produced
  with the chapter, not after the class.

#### The ladder, measured end to end — and class 5 makes it stack

Every figure below is the median of each grade's chapter medians, over the plans actually shipped:

| | class 3 | class 4 | class 5 | class 6 | class 7 | class 8 | class 9 | class 10 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| chapters measured | **1** | *0* | 2 | 13 | 10 | 10 | 12 | 5 |
| `explanation` | **44** | *~55 target* | **66** | 76 | 103 | 118 | **165** | **132** |
| `real_life_example` | **21** | *~24 target* | **27** | 72 | 81 | 64 | ~95 | ~44 |

Class 4 is italicised because it is a **target, not a measurement** — nothing has been built at
that grade. Replace it with the pilot's medians and drop the italics; a target left standing as if
it were a measurement is how the class-9/class-10 inversion survived.

**3 → 5 → 6 → 7 → 8 → 9 now steps up monotonically** and class 4's target sits between 44 and 66
with room on both sides. The class-10 inversion documented under §Class 10 is unchanged and is
still the open decision — a class-10 calibration problem, not a primary one.

**5 → 6 → 7 → 8 → 9 now steps up monotonically**, and class 5 was authored to land there rather
than being fitted to it afterwards. The class-10 inversion documented under §Class 10 is unchanged
and is still the open decision — it is a class-10 calibration problem, not a class-5 one.

`real_life_example` does **not** step monotonically at any grade and never has; it tracks what the
chapter's anchor needs, not the grade. Class 5's 27 is deliberate — at ten, one clear sentence
about घर or खेल does the work that a paragraph does at twelve.

### Class 6 — eleven to twelve

Simple खड़ी बोली, short sentences, second-person teacher voice. Technical terms in Devanagari with
the English in brackets on first use only: `मानवीकरण (personification)`. Full calibration in
`reference/teaching_voice_hi.md`.

### Class 7 — twelve to thirteen

The same voice, moved up one step. Four concrete differences, and they are differences of
**demand**, not of vocabulary level — do not simply use harder words.

| | class 6 | class 7 |
|---|---|---|
| `explanation` | 55–90 words | **70–110 words** |
| `real_life_example` | one everyday anchor, close to home — घर, कक्षा, मोहल्ला | may reach **beyond the child's own street** — school, town, a public situation, something read or watched |
| craft naming | name the device, show it once | name it **and ask what it does to the meaning** |
| the closing question | "क्या तुम्हारे साथ ऐसा हुआ है?" | may ask for a **judgement or a choice**, not only a memory |

What does **not** change: Devanagari throughout, Roman only inside brackets for a technical term,
second-person teacher voice, no lecturing, and no assumed prior knowledge of the literary form.

A class-7 child can hold a longer chunk and an abstraction they cannot yet see an example of —
so `प्रश्न किस काम का है` (साक्षात्कार) and `कविता अपने पहले शब्द पर क्यों लौटती है` (कुंडलिया)
are reasonable asks at this band and would not have been at class 6.

### Class 8 — thirteen to fourteen

One more step of demand. The specific new capacity is **holding two texts against each other** —
and मल्हार 8 asks for exactly that: eight of its ten chapters close with a साझी समझ that compares
the main text with the झरोखे से printed after it.

| | class 7 | class 8 |
|---|---|---|
| `explanation` | 70–110 words | **85–130 words** |
| `real_life_example` | may reach beyond the child's own street | may reach a **social or historical** situation the child has not lived — a freedom movement, a court, a rented house |
| craft naming | name it and ask what it does | name it, ask what it does, and ask **why this device and not another** |
| the closing question | may ask for a judgement or a choice | may ask the child to **compare two texts**, or to hold two views at once |

What does **not** change, at any band: Devanagari throughout, Roman only inside brackets on first
use of a technical term, second-person teacher voice, no lecturing, no assumed prior knowledge of
the literary form.

A class-8 reader also meets **old and dialectal Hindi as objects of study rather than obstacles** —
भारतेंदु's 1871 prose in हरिद्वार, कबीर's `हिरदे` / `लागौं` / `सुभाय`. The verbatim stays exactly
as printed (`reference/devanagari_verbatim.md`); the modernising happens in `explanation` and
`key_terms`, never in `original_chunk`.

### Class 9 — fourteen to fifteen

One more step again. This band was **written after four chapters had already been built to it**
(ch8 पद, ch6 रीढ़ की हड्डी, ch9 राम-लक्ष्मण-परशुराम संवाद, ch10 भारति, जय, विजयकरे!), so the
numbers below are **measured from those plans, not chosen in advance**. Anything built earlier
that sits outside the range is listed at the bottom rather than quietly re-fitted.

| | class 8 | class 9 |
|---|---|---|
| `explanation` | 85–130 words | **125–175 words** (measured medians 134 / 146 / 154 / 163) |
| `real_life_example` | a social or historical situation the child has not lived | **85–120 words**, and it may now be an *idea* rather than a situation — a principle, a professional practice, a way of arguing |
| craft naming | name it, ask what it does, ask why this device | name it, and ask **what the text would lose if the device were removed** |
| the closing question | compare two texts, or hold two views at once | **weigh two positions and commit to one, with the text as evidence** — `evaluate` appears as a real bloom level here, not a stretch |

The new capacity is **arguing from the text against a plausible alternative reading.** गंगा asks
for this directly. Chapter 9 sets राम's विनय against लक्ष्मण's तर्क and makes the student choose;
chapter 6 turns on whether उमा's outburst is justified or merely angry. Both questions have a
defensible answer on each side, which is exactly what makes them class-9 questions and not
class-8 ones. A class-8 comparison question asks *how are these two texts different*; a class-9
one asks *which of these two is right, and how do you know from the page*.

Three things carry over unchanged and one changes:

- Unchanged: Devanagari throughout, Roman only inside brackets on first use of a technical term,
  second-person teacher voice, no lecturing, no assumed prior knowledge of the literary form.
- Changed: **अवधी, ब्रज and सधुक्कड़ी arrive as whole texts, not as quoted lines.** Class 8 met
  कबीर's `हिरदे` inside a modern frame; class 9 reads three full अवधी chapters. The `explanation`
  now routinely has to carry the gloss of a form the reader has never seen — `चितवहिं`, `जैहहिं`,
  `बास समानी` — while `original_chunk` stays exactly as printed
  (`reference/devanagari_verbatim.md`).

**Known deviation:** class-8 ch8 (नए मेहमान) ran to a median of 149 words, above its own band and
inside class 9's. It is an एकांकी with long दृश्य-प्रसंग units and the length follows the unit, not
the grade. Left as shipped.

### Class 10 — fifteen to sixteen

A board year, and the first band where the reader is expected to **hold two readings of a line at
once**. The voice stays the pack's — second person, Devanagari, no lecturing — but four demands
move up, and none of them is "use harder words".

Class 10 was **authored before the class-8 and class-9 bands existed** (the two grades were built
on separate machines), so it was calibrated against class 7 and the table below is left in the
terms it was written in. Read the reconciliation note under it before using the numbers.

| | class 7 | class 10 |
|---|---|---|
| `explanation` | 70–110 words | **100–150 words** |
| `real_life_example` | school, town, a public situation | may be **social or historical**, and may ask the reader to judge rather than to recall |
| craft naming | name the device and ask what it does | **analyse** — say what the device does *and* what the text would lose without it |
| the closing question | a judgement or a choice | may ask the reader to **weigh two readings** and defend one |

What does not change: Devanagari throughout, Roman only inside brackets for a technical term, no
assumed prior knowledge of the literary form, and no moralising.

A class-10 reader can be asked *"यह वाक्य नाटक में दो बार आता है — दोनों बार का अर्थ एक है या
अलग?"* or *"जो आदमी नहीं जानता कि दिन कैसे ख़त्म होगा, वह उसे कैसे लिखता है?"* Neither is a
reasonable ask at class 7, and both are ordinary here.

**The अभ्यास is not optional polish at this band.** स्पर्श's प्रश्न-अभ्यास and भाषा अध्ययन are
what a student is actually examined on, and `exercise_solutions.json` should be produced with the
chapter, not after the class.

#### Reconciliation with class 9 — the ladder inverts, and it is measured, not assumed

Merging the class-10 work into this tree put the two bands side by side for the first time. They
do not stack. Measured over every shipped `explanation` in each grade's built plans:

| | chapters | median of chapter medians | chapter medians span | stated band |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| class 9 | 12 | **165** | 134–198 | 125–175 |
| class 10 | 5 | **132** | 115–157 | 100–150 |

Class 10 as actually built runs **shorter than class 9**, and `real_life_example` is not close —
class 9 medians run 77–118 words, class 10's run **36–52**. Each grade is internally consistent
and each sits inside its own stated band, so nothing shipped is out of spec; the bands themselves
disagree.

The likely cause is the authoring history above: class 10 stepped up from class 7 and skipped the
two rungs in between, which are precisely where the length grows. **Neither band has been rewritten
here** — the numbers are what real plans measure, and re-fitting one to the other would mean
editing shipped chapters to match a ladder nobody has decided on. Decide the intended shape before
the next class-10 chapter is authored; ch12 onward will otherwise keep the class-7-derived
calibration.

## Ids

```
chapter_id = cbse_eng_hindi{grade}_ch{unit_number}      → cbse_eng_hindi6_ch1
plan_id    = {chapter_id}_v{version}                    → cbse_eng_hindi6_ch1_v1
subject    = "Hindi"        board = "cbse"       level = "standard"
```

**The `eng` is the medium, not the subject, and it is not negotiable.** `cbse_hin_hindi6_ch1`
appears in older notes and in an earlier draft of `reference/phase2_contract.md`; it is wrong.
Checked against the live server — `GET /api/lp2/learning-plans/chapter/cbse_eng_hindi6_ch1`
returns the shipped class-6 plan, and the `cbse_hin_…` form 404s. `chapter_master_map.json` uses
the `cbse_eng_…` form for every grade. The full failure mode — it uploads clean and goes live
under the wrong medium — is in `reference/naming_conventions.md`.

### Which root ids the author sets, and which the server does

**The author sets exactly two: `chapter_master_id` and `publication_id`**, both from
`learning_plan_upload/chapter_master_map.json`, which carries only those two per chapter (plus
`path`) across all 560 entries.

**`subject_ref_id` and `medium_id` are owned by the server**, not the plan. They sit in
`lp_sync/config.py:VOLATILE_TOP_LEVEL_KEYS` alongside `plan_id`, `version` and `_activate` —
the list the local↔remote comparison drops precisely because the server fills them. Both
reference V2 plans on disk (`data/V2/…/cbse_eng_eng7_ch1.json`, `…englishgrammar5_ch1.json`)
carry `"subject_ref_id": null, "medium_id": null`, and the LP2 validator accepts `null`.

**They are not derivable, so do not guess one.** Reading a shipped plan back from the API is
misleading: class 6 comes back `84`, class 7 `78`, class 8 `85`. That looks like a table to copy
from; it follows no pattern, and inventing a value is worse than leaving it null.

**Where a real subject record exists, send the real values.** Class-10 Hindi is
`subject_ref_id: 90`, `medium_id: 1` — from the subject record, not from the pattern:

```jsonc
{"subjectId": 90, "subjectName": "Hindi", "boardId": 1, "boardName": "CBSE",
 "gradeId": 10, "gradeName": "Class 10", "mediumId": 1, "mediumName": "English",
 "publicationId": 1, "publicationName": "CBSE", "year": 2025}
```

Class 9 is the confirming data point from the other direction: `subject_ref_id: 91` was sent and
**91 came back unnormalised**. So a real value survives; only an invented one is a hazard. Without
such a record for a new grade, write `null` and let the server fill it.

`estimated_time` **is** the author's: 1.5 at classes 6–7, 2 at class 10.

## Class-10 source PDFs — precompose the nuqta, and NFC will not do it

Two of fourteen 404 on the `textbook_url` recorded in their own plan — ch6 कर चले हम फ़िदा and
ch8 बड़े भाई साहब. The bucket stores the **precomposed** nukta letter (फ़ U+095E, ड़ U+095C); the
plan records the **decomposed** pair.

**`unicodedata.normalize("NFC", …)` does not fix this.** Devanagari nukta letters are Unicode
composition exclusions, so NFC leaves the pair as it was and the 404 survives a normalisation
that looks like it handled the problem. Use the explicit singleton map in
`output10/_fetch_pages10.py:precompose()`, built by *decomposing* each precomposed character —
a hand-typed `ड़` in a source file is not necessarily the bucket's `ड़`, which is the whole bug.

This is the third distinct filename failure mode in the pack, after class 7's romanised/decomposed
names and class 8's doubled मात्राएँ. The rule they share holds here too: **never conclude a PDF
is missing from a 404.**

Plans also disagree about encoding — ch6 stores its URL already percent-encoded, ch8 stores raw
Devanagari — so **unquote first, always, then encode once.** Both
`storage.googleapis.com/singularity_frontend_assest/…` and
`media.singularity-learn.com/singularity_frontend_assest/…` serve identical bytes.

And a 200 still proves nothing about *which* chapter you got (class 7's ch3 served chapter 2's
PDF at 200): **the URL is a hint, page 1 is the proof.**

### ch8 goes further: the *word* is not a stable unit either

ch8 needed twenty corrections and broke the last assumption standing — that once a word is
checked, its spelling holds for the chapter. स्पर्श prints the same word both ways within a few
pages:

| word | one place | another |
|---|---|---|
| मिहनत / मेहनत | **मिहनत** p46, inside भाई साहब's speech | **मेहनत** p46b, p48, p49b |
| लिहाज़ / लिहाज | **लिहाज़** p50a | **लिहाज** p50b — the facing half of one page |
| न रहा। / न बचा। | **वह रौब मुझ पर न रहा।** p48 | **पानी देने वाला भी न बचा।** p48, same page |

So a `VERBATIM_FIXES` table applied with `str.replace()` is itself a hazard: fixing v1's wrong
`मिहनत` corrupts the one that was right, four pages earlier. **Corrections must be scoped to
their sentence.** ch8 therefore carries no fixes table at all — the corrections are applied in
place in `output10/ch8/printed_text.md`, each next to the page it was read from, and nothing
rewrites the text after parsing.

शब्दों का खेल · बूझो तो जानें · समझिए और लिखिए · पता कीजिए · पता कीजिए और लिखिए · खोजिए और लिखिए ·
कल्पना कीजिए · मेरी सोच · मेरी कलाकारी · कला की कलाकारियाँ · कुछ नया करें · कुछ बनाइए · बनाइए ·
खेल-खेल में · खेल-खेल में खोजें · वर्ग पहेली · खेल गीत · आइए मिलकर गाएँ · आइए बनाएँ ·
आइए पौधा लगाएँ · आनंद के लिए, plus the **विधा-indexed** blocks below.

Four things are true of class 3 and of no other grade:

1. **The block is named after the विधा of the text it follows.** A poem gets
   *कविता की बात · कविता से · कविता से आगे*; a story gets *कहानी की बात · कहानी से · कहानी से आगे*;
   prose gets *पाठ के भीतर · पाठ के आगे*. The banner is therefore a **second, independent witness to
   the विधा**, and it has agreed with the opener badge everywhere it has been checked.
2. **`कविता से आगे` / `कहानी से आगे` / `पढ़िए और जानिए` / `इन्हें भी जानिए` / `आइए जानें` are reading
   matter, not exercises** — the same call class 5 makes for `इसे भी जानिए`. They are topics, and
   phase 1 classified them `CONCEPT`, which is right.
3. **`शिक्षण-संकेत` is addressed to the teacher**, printed in a tinted box — ch7 p62:
   *"विद्यार्थियों से अंतर्देशीय पत्र, पोस्टकार्ड, डाक टिकट … के बारे में बातचीत कीजिए।"* It is
   neither a topic nor an अभ्यास item. Inventory it in `01_meta.json` and drop it; a solution
   written for it would be answering the teacher.
4. **`खेल गीत` is a badge on one chapter's opener and a banner in another chapter's अभ्यास.**
   ch10 p82 badges रस्साकशी with it; ch7 p62 uses it to head *"ऐसे ही कोई दो गीत गाइए और लिखिए—"*.
   Same words, opposite role, and only the render distinguishes them: **a badge sits on a chapter
   opener beside the QR block.**

**वीणा (class 4)** — बातचीत के लिए (13) · भाषा की बात (10) · सोचिए और लिखिए (9) · पाठ के भीतर (7) ·
अनुमान और कल्पना · पाठ से आगे · कलाकारी · कविता की बात · कविता के आधार पर · कविता से आगे ·
कविता मंचन · समझ और अनुभव · समझ की बात · अपनी बात · मिलान कीजिए · शब्द-पिटारा · शब्द खेल ·
चित्रों की भाषा · आनंदमयी गतिविधि · बूझो तो जानें · अभिनय · भूल-भुलैया ·
पुस्तकालय या अन्य स्रोत से.

Five of those — बातचीत के लिए, सोचिए और लिखिए, भाषा की बात, अनुमान और कल्पना, समझ और अनुभव — are
class 5's list verbatim; `पाठ के भीतर` and `पाठ से आगे` are class 3's prose pair surviving one grade
up. **Class 4 is the join in the series**, and it sits much closer to class 5 than to class 3.

Both primary grades inherit class 5's rules 4 and 5 below — the rotated puzzle answer, and *do not
take the block list from a banner scan*. Class 3 makes the second one worse: its banners are
graphics beside their block, and whole exercises are **pictures with no text layer at all** (ch1's
first item is a picture-to-word मिलान). Read the block list off the renders.

**वीणा (class 5)** — बातचीत के लिए · पाठ से · **पाठ से आगे** · सोचिए और लिखिए · समझ और अनुभव ·
अनुमान और कल्पना · भाषा की बात · पुस्तकालय एवं अन्य स्रोत · **पता लगाकर कीजिए** ·
**बूझो तो जानें / बूझो पहेली** · आपकी अभिव्यक्ति, plus recurring named activities —
कल्पना की उड़ान, तुलना, घूमो-देखो, आपकी चित्रकारी, मैं भी सिद्धार्थ, मेरा पक्षी, **नाटक-मंचन**,
**भूल-भुलैया**, and per-chapter one-offs named after the chapter itself (**मेरा न्याय** in ch2,
**सुंदरिया और हीरासिंह** in ch5).

Five things about वीणा's apparatus are not true of any other series and each costs a block or a
topic if missed:

1. **`इसे भी जानिए` is reading matter, not an exercise.** In ch1 it is a 144-word explanation of
   how day and night happen; in ch12 a 120-word introduction to कृत्रिम मेधा. It is a **topic**,
   and phase 1 already classified both as `CONCEPT` rather than `EXERCISE` — the right call.
   Cut it under `soochnatmak_sanskritik`.
2. **`पाठ से` recurs.** It is a banner reused two and three times down a chapter (ch4 on book
   pp. 37, 38 and 40), not a single block. Collect **all** of its occurrences.
3. **The apparatus is larger than the text** in every chapter of the book — ch1 is one page of poem
   against four of अभ्यास, ch6 two against nine. `exercise_solutions.json` is the larger of the two
   deliverables here and is produced with the chapter.
4. **A puzzle page usually prints its own answer, rotated 180°, in a bottom corner.** Four found so
   far, and the rule has held every time it was checked: ch2 p20 (*उत्तर – छाया, तरबूज, मोर, गन्ना,
   सूर्य* for the five बोली पहेलियाँ), ch5 p57 (*उत्तर – कोयल, कछुआ, नाव*), ch5 p59 (*उत्तर – चींटी
   ने हेलमेट पहन रखा था।*), ch10 p126 (*उत्तर – अंक 2 और 4 की मछलियाँ*). It is **not** in the text
   layer in usable order and it is easy to miss on a render. Crop the corner, rotate it, and read
   it before answering any वीणा puzzle from first principles — the book's own answer is the answer.
5. **Do not take the block list from a banner scan.** `output5/_blocks5.py` finds banners by
   matching known names in the text layer, and it mislabelled **पाठ से आगे** as a second
   **पाठ से** on *both* ch2 p11 and ch5 p10, and missed पुस्तकालय एवं अन्य स्रोत, पता लगाकर कीजिए
   and बूझो तो जानें entirely because their banners sit inside decorative panels. Treat that scan
   as a hint about *where* to look; read the block list off the renders.

वीणा's **भाषा की बात is vocabulary noticing**, not the graded grammar स्पर्श's भाषा अध्ययन carries:
समानार्थी, विलोम, मुहावरे, संज्ञा/विशेषण/क्रिया identification, विराम चिह्न, द्विरुक्ति. It is much
closer to मल्हार's block than to स्पर्श's — see `reference/bhasha_bodh.md` §Class 5.

**मल्हार (classes 6–8).** Classes 6–7: मेरी समझ से · सोच-विचार के लिए · भाषा की बात ·
कविता की रचना · आपकी बात · मिलकर करें मिलान · कहानी से आगे · शब्द-संदर्भ.

अनुमान-कल्पना · पाठ से आगे · आना-जाना · डायरी · खोजबीन के लिए · साझी समझ · आज की पहेली ·
परियोजना कार्य · जीवन कौशल · सावधानी · शब्दकोश.

**गंगा (class 9)** carries its own banners — विषयों से संवाद, भाषा से संवाद, सृजन, झरोखे से —
and they are **graphics with no text layer**, so they cannot be found by searching. See the
