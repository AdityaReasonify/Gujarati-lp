# संवाद / निबंध

## Diagnosis signals
Two voices in sustained conversation, or single-voice prose making a **case**. No plot to speak
of; the movement is in the argument. Theme: पर्यावरण, समाज, ज़िम्मेदारी. अभ्यास asks the child what
each side says, whether they agree, and often asks them to write or debate their own view.

## Lens
**तर्क + दृष्टिकोण + ज़िम्मेदारी** — two positions, and what they ask of the reader.

## Guiding question
*दोनों पक्ष क्या कहते हैं, और मुझसे क्या चाहते हैं?*

## Explanation unit — **one तर्क, or one exchange that completes a thought**

For संवाद: an exchange that completes one point — a question and the answer that settles it. Do
not cut mid-argument; do not bundle three unrelated points into one topic.
For निबंध: one strand of the case.

Both speakers' words stay verbatim in `original_chunk`, with speaker labels as printed.

## Emphasise
- **Both sides, fairly.** Even where the chapter clearly favours one, the other position is
  stated as its holder would state it. A flattened opponent teaches the child that arguing is
  about winning.
- **The move each turn makes** — objecting, conceding, giving evidence, asking. Naming the move
  teaches argument as a skill.
- **What the text asks of the reader.** These chapters usually end in a responsibility. State it
  as the text does.
- **मानवीकरण where a non-human speaks.** A tree that argues is a device, and the child should see
  the choice: giving the tree a voice makes its case impossible to ignore.
- **Everyday reasoning.** The best hand-over is showing the same argument in the child's own life.

## Avoid (hard gate)
- **One-sided preaching.** The most common failure: turning a two-voice chapter into a lecture on
  what the child must do.
- **Flattening the opposing voice** into stupidity or villainy.
- **Losing the संवाद form** by summarising both speakers into one narration.
- **Guilt.** Environmental chapters especially — hand the child something they can do, not a
  weight they cannot lift.

## Priors

**Media** — one image per exchange, showing **both parties together** in the same frame so the
conversation is visible. Where a non-human speaks, draw it with presence but without a cartoon
face unless the chapter's own tone supports it.

**अभ्यास** — expect "what does each say", agree/disagree with reasons, vocabulary, and a
debate or write-your-own-view task. Where a topic prepares the debate, its explanation should
name the move the child will need (giving a reason, answering an objection).

**Language** — accessible modern Hindi, sometimes with domain vocabulary (पर्यावरण, संसाधन,
संतुलन). Gloss at point of use.

## Recall priors
One `remember` (what does each side claim), one `understand` (what evidence is given), one
`evaluate` (which side convinces you, and why).
