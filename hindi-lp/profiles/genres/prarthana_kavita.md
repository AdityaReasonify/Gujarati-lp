# प्रार्थना-कविता

> Written for class-10 स्पर्श ch7 `आत्मत्राण` (रवींद्रनाथ ठाकुर, अनुवाद हजारीप्रसाद द्विवेदी).
> The chapter fell through the whole verse branch of `_genre_index.md` and landed on
> `prakriti_deshbhakti_kavita`, whose lens is *चित्र + भाव + अलंकार — a feeling built out of
> pictures*. आत्मत्राण has almost no pictures. It is abstract petition, and the borrowed lens
> would have produced a chapter hunting for imagery that is not there.

## Diagnosis signals

**Verse addressed to God, in the second person, asking for something.** The whole poem is one
sustained request — `बचाओ`, `न दो`, `होवे`, `रखना` — and there is a name or epithet for the
listener that keeps returning: `करुणामय`, `प्रभु`, `हे नाथ`.

The decisive signal is **what is asked for**. A प्रार्थना-कविता at this level does not ask for
rescue; it asks for the strength to face what is coming. आत्मत्राण says so in its first line and
then says it five more times: *विपदाओं से मुझे बचाओ, यह मेरी प्रार्थना नहीं*. The poem is built
out of that refusal — **"not this, but this"** — repeated until it becomes a position.

Four things separate it from its neighbours, and each would have taken this chapter:

| looks like | but | tell |
|---|---|---|
| `bhakti_pad` | a पद is **sung**, has a टेक that returns whole, ब्रज/अवधी forms, and a कवि-छाप in the last line | none of those are here; and भक्ति-पद's relationship is *love or वात्सल्य*, while this one is closer to a contract |
| `prakriti_deshbhakti_kavita` | that विधा builds feeling out of **pictures** — nature, land, season | this builds it out of **grammar**: negation, condition, contrast. There is nothing to draw |
| `niti_doha` | a दोहा tells the reader how to live | a प्रार्थना tells **God** what the speaker intends. The addressee is the whole difference |
| `mukt_chhand_kavita` | free verse has no repeating unit | this rhymes hard and repeatedly (भय / जय / क्षय / निर्भय / संशय) and its stanzas answer each other |

**Translation is normal here and is part of the subject.** These poems often arrive through a
translator, and the translator is usually named on the page.

## Lens

**याचना + शर्त + आत्मबल** — what is asked, what is refused, and the strength the speaker is
asking to be given rather than spared.

## Guiding question

*कवि जो माँग सकता था वह नहीं माँगता — तो वह माँगता क्या है, और क्यों?*

## Explanation unit — **one छंद, or one complete petition**

A petition is a *"not this — but this"* pair and it must stay whole. The refusal alone is half a
sentence and teaches nothing; granting alone sounds like the very rescue the poem refuses.

Two short छंद that complete one petition may join. A छंद that carries two distinct petitions may
split. Decide from where the pair closes, not from the line count.

**Do not cut between the negative and its positive.** `विपदाओं से मुझे बचाओ, यह मेरी प्रार्थना
नहीं` / `केवल इतना हो … कभी न विपदा में पाऊँ भय` is one unit. Splitting it produces a topic in
which the poet appears to refuse help and stop there.

## Emphasise

- **The refusal is the argument.** Every stanza starts by giving something up — rescue, comfort,
  a lighter load, being spared. Name what is being refused each time; the poem's shape is the
  list of things the speaker will not ask for.
- **The repeated address.** `करुणामय` returns like a टेक but is not one — it is a form of address,
  and it keeps the poem a conversation rather than a resolution. Ask why the poet keeps naming
  the listener.
- **Grammar doing the work of imagery.** `नहीं … पर`, `अगर … तो भी`, `न … केवल`. Where another
  विधा would give a picture, this gives a condition. That is worth naming out loud, because a
  child trained on nature poems will look for scenery and find none.
- **Self-reliance inside devotion, not against it.** The speaker never doubts the listener's
  power; he declines to use it. Teach it as a relationship being defined, not as scepticism —
  and never as "God is not needed."
- **The translator's presence.** Where the page names a translator, that is content: ask what a
  translator has to keep (the argument, the rhyme, the address) and what cannot survive the
  crossing. `reference/devanagari_verbatim.md` still governs — the translation is the text.

## Avoid (hard gate)

- **Turning the prayer into a moral.** It is not "God helps those who help themselves." The poem
  is a request, not a proverb, and a tacked-on शिक्षा destroys the form.
- **Cutting a petition in half** — the refusal without its grant, or the grant without its refusal.
- **Reading the refusal as doubt or as rejection of faith.** The poem asks *more* of the listener,
  not less: it asks to be changed rather than protected.
- **Supplying pictures the poem does not have** to make it look like a nature poem.
- **Flattening the address.** `करुणामय` is not decoration and may not be paraphrased away.
- **Treating a printed variant as a mistake.** स्पर्श prints `(करुणामय)` three times and
  `(करुणायम)` once; both stand as printed (`reference/devanagari_verbatim.md`).

## Priors

**Media** — the hardest विधा in the pack to illustrate, because the poem refuses scenes. Prefer a
single figure in a posture the poem names — `नत शिर होकर`, standing unhelped, bearing a load — over
any attempt to draw the abstraction. Do not draw God. Existing chapter art for these poems tends to
be sparse and generic; match on `generation_prompt` and accept fewer frames rather than forcing one.

**अभ्यास** — expect questions on what the poet does *not* ask for, on the meaning of a single
word (`त्राण`, `अनुनय`, `वंचना`), and on whether the reader agrees with the poet's position.
स्पर्श also asks about the translation itself.

**Language** — तत्सम-heavy and compact: `त्राण`, `अनामय`, `अनुनय`, `निखिल मही`, `क्षय`. Gloss
generously; the difficulty here is vocabulary, not syntax.

## Recall priors

One `remember` (what is asked / what is refused), one `understand` (why that exchange), one
`analyze` or `evaluate` (what the poem would lose if the refusal were dropped; whether the reader
would ask for the same thing).
