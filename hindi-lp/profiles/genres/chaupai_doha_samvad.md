# चौपाई-दोहा संवाद (प्रबंध-काव्य का संवाद-प्रसंग)

> Written for **गंगा कक्षा 9, पाठ 9 — राम-लक्ष्मण-परशुराम संवाद** (तुलसीदास, *रामचरितमानस*,
> बालकांड)। First use. It exists because none of the three verse profiles that look close
> actually fit; see *Why the neighbouring profiles fail* below.

## Diagnosis signals

**Structure (A).** Verse laid out as a **run of चौपाई closed by a दोहा**, and then another run,
and another. Each चौपाई line prints as one line with a single दंड `।` at its midpoint and a
double दंड `॥` at its end — two अर्धालियाँ on one printed line. The दोहा is set **centred and
indented**, two lines, and always ends a run. The book's own भाषा-से-संवाद page may name this:
गंगा's ch9 lists `दोहा-चौपाई का क्रम से होना` as a feature of the poem.

**Theme (B).** Narrative, not lyric. Named characters arrive, speak, threaten, answer. There is a
**कथावाचक** who reports what people did and felt (`सोचहिं सकल त्रास उर भारी`), and inside that
report there are **direct speeches with no speaker tag** — the reader must work out who is
speaking from the surrounding line.

**अभ्यास (C).** Asks *whose statement is this?* (a table of कथन × पात्र), *what mood does this
line show and in which character?*, and *turn this चौपाई into गद्य-रूप*. It does **not** ask
भाव-स्पष्ट करो per couplet (that is `niti_doha`) and it does **not** ask about a टेक (that is
`bhakti_pad`).

**Purpose (D).** Character built through speech. The chapter is titled a **संवाद** and the book
says so plainly: *संवाद ही चरित्र का निर्माण करते हैं*.

**Language.** अवधी (or ब्रज for other प्रबंध-काव्य), with a glossary page that is usually long —
गंगा ch9's शब्द-संपदा glosses 38 words for 27 lines of verse.

### Why the neighbouring profiles fail

| profile | its unit | why it breaks here |
|---|---|---|
| `bhakti_pad` | one whole पद | there is no पद and no टेक; this is narrative, not a sung utterance of devotion |
| `niti_doha` | one दोहा | the दोहा here is **not self-contained** — it closes a चौपाई run and often hands the speech over to the next one. Taken alone it is unreadable |
| `vir_ras_kavita` / `prakriti_deshbhakti_kavita` | one छंद | "one छंद" resolves to one चौपाई (four चरण). 24 चौपाइयाँ would become 24 topics of two printed lines each |
| `samvad_nibandh`, `ekanki`, `sakshatkar` | prose units | there is no पात्र-परिचय, no रंग-निर्देश, no interviewer; and a कथावाचक is present, which a play forbids |

## Lens

**प्रसंग + पात्र + वचन** — what situation the excerpt has walked into, who is standing in it, and
what each one's words do to it.

## Guiding question

*एक ही सभा में खड़े लोग एक ही घटना पर इतने अलग-अलग ढंग से क्यों बोलते हैं?*

## Explanation unit — **one संवाद-विनिमय, अपनी चौपाई-दोहा इकाई के भीतर**

A **विनिमय** is a statement and the response it draws: a speech and its reply, or an event and the
सभा's reaction to it. The unit has two halves and both must be in the same topic.

Two boundary rules, and they are hard:

1. **A topic never crosses a दोहा.** The दोहा seals the run it closes; it belongs to the topic
   whose movement it completes, never to the run that follows. This is the rule that makes the
   form's own architecture visible to the child.
2. **Inside a चौपाई-दोहा run, a boundary may fall only where the विनिमय completes** — the end of a
   speech, or the point where the narration turns to a new party. Never in the middle of a speech,
   and never at a line count.

Applied to a run of eight चौपाइयाँ + दोहा this normally yields **two topics**, not one and not
eight. A run that genuinely holds only one exchange stays one topic and puts its turns in
`concepts[]`.

Two consequences worth stating because they look wrong at first:

- **A दोहा that introduces the next speech still belongs to the previous topic.**
  `हृदयँ न हरषु बिषादु कछु बोले श्रीरघुबीरु` is the *end* of the fear-movement (everyone is
  afraid; राम alone is not), even though the words राम speaks are printed in the next run. The
  explanation of the following topic must open by naming the bridge.
- **A narration-only stretch is still a विनिमय.** परशुराम arrives and the सभा rises in terror;
  nobody speaks, but a statement has been made and answered.

## Emphasise

- **Speaker attribution, every time.** These verses drop the speaker tag: `बोले परसुधरहि अपमाने`
  tells you लक्ष्मण spoke and परशुराम was insulted, in five words, with no colon and no name for
  the speaker. Say who is speaking in every topic; an unattributed line is the single most common
  way this विधा is mistaught.
- **The many names of one person.** भृगुपति · परसुधर · भृगुकुलकेतू · मुनि · गोसाईं are all
  परशुराम; तिपुरारि is शिव; रघुबीर is राम. The book's own व्याकरण page makes this the lesson.
  Name the effect too — a संबोधन chosen for the line's lineage or its ओज, not for variety.
- **The चौपाई's internal caesura.** The single `।` splits each line into two अर्धालियाँ, and the
  turn very often sits exactly there: `अति डरु उतरु देत नृपु नाहीं। कुटिल भूप हरषे मन माहीं॥` —
  fear on one side, glee on the other, in one line.
- **The दोहा as a clincher.** It compresses and it judges. Point at what the दोहा does that the
  चौपाइयाँ could not.
- **अवधी as an object of study, never an obstacle.** `कै` = किसने, `जैहहिं` = जाएँगे,
  `चितवहिं` = देखते हैं. Gloss in `shabdarth`; never modernise `original_chunk`.
- **The excerpt's edges.** These chapters are cut out of a महाकाव्य. Say what happened just before
  the first line and what the textbook itself says happens after the last one; the child cannot
  infer it.

## Avoid (hard gate)

- **Cutting by दोहा alone** (one दोहा = one topic) or **by चौपाई** (one line = one topic). The
  first orphans the दोहा from its run; the second shreds the poem.
- **Splitting a speech from the reply it draws.** Exactly the failure `sakshatkar` guards against.
- **Merging a whole चौपाई-दोहा run into one topic** when it holds two exchanges. A run is a
  printing unit, not automatically a teaching unit.
- **Retelling the प्रसंग as a prose story** and dropping the verse's own order of revelation.
- **Naming an अलंकार the page does not support.** Where the book prints its own अलंकार table, use
  those examples first.
- **Turning the character into a moral.** परशुराम is not "anger is bad"; he is a man whose
  authority has just been ignored in public.
- **Modernising or "correcting" अवधी spellings** — `बेषु`, `सिरु`, `नृपु`, `धनुही`, `मुसुकाने`
  are as printed.

## Priors

**Media** — one image per विनिमय, composed as a **सभा picture**: who is standing where in the
hall, whose hand is raised, who is bowing, where the broken bow lies. The hall stays the same
across the chapter, so a frame set anywhere else is a wrong match. Existing art for these chapters
is dense but **often wrong about who is present** — check every named figure against the excerpt
before reusing a frame (a Rāmāyaṇa scene attracts characters from the rest of the epic).

**अभ्यास** — expect MCQs on mood-per-line, a कथन × पात्र matching table, गद्य-रूप conversion of a
चौपाई, an अलंकार table the book fills in halfway, बहुभाषिकता (अवधी → खड़ी बोली → अपनी भाषा), and a
staging activity (रामलीला / कठपुतली). None of these is a topic.

**Language band** — the verse is hard and the glossary is long; put the शब्द-संपदा gloss into
`shabdarth` verbatim and let `modified_chunk` carry a plain-Hindi rendering line by line, because
the child's first need here is simply to know what the line says.

## Recall priors

One `remember` (who said it / what happened), one `understand` (what the line means, or why the
speaker says it that way), one `analyze` or `evaluate` (what the दोहा adds, or how two characters'
responses to the same situation differ).
