# स्मृति-विरह गीत

*(लंबी तुकांत गीत-कविता — स्मृति, बिछोह और संबोधन)*

## Diagnosis signals

Verse, and **strictly metrical** — so the मुक्त छंद test fails at once. Short lines of near-equal
length (typically 16 मात्रा, a चौपाई-like गति), grouped in **many small equal स्तबक** — usually
four lines, rhyming in pairs (अ अ ब ब). The rhyme is dense and audible; often it is a
*doubled* rhyme (`गिर रहा है / घिर रहा है`, `टूट बैठा / फूट बैठा`) rather than a single syllable.

Four further signals, and it is the **combination** that routes here:

1. **The poem is long — thirty, forty, fifty स्तबक** — far longer than a छंद-wise poem the
   पाठ्यपुस्तक expects to be taught stanza by stanza.
2. **The स्तबक run on.** A sentence begins in one and closes two later; a portrait of one person
   occupies three or four. The stanza break is a breath, **not** a unit of sense.
3. **A speaker separated from someone, remembering them.** Jail, exile, hostel, a journey, death.
   The present tense is thin and the remembered world is thick.
4. **संबोधन** — the poem talks *to* something that cannot answer: the सावन, the हवा, the बादल,
   the मन, the absent family. Often that address returns at the end, closing a ring.

**Tested after `mukt_chhand_kavita` and before the छंद-wise profiles**
(`prakriti_deshbhakti_kavita`, `vir_ras_kavita`). It is not मुक्त छंद — the metre and rhyme are
strict. But the छंद-wise profiles would give one topic per स्तबक, and on a fifty-स्तबक poem that
produces fifty topics and destroys every portrait by cutting it in three. Ask, after confirming
there **is** a repeating unit: *is the unit of sense the same size as the unit of metre?* Here it
is not, and that mismatch is the whole diagnosis.

## Lens

**स्मृति + बिंब + संबोधन** — what is being remembered, the picture memory arrives in, and who the
poem is speaking to.

## Guiding question

*कवि जो सचमुच कह रहा है और जो कहलवाना चाहता है — उनमें फ़र्क़ क्यों है?*

## Explanation unit — **one भाव-खंड (छंद-समूह)**

A **भाव-खंड** is a run of consecutive स्तबक held together by **one figure or one movement of
feeling**, and it ends where the poem turns to someone else or to something else. This is the
same unit `mukt_chhand_kavita` uses, and for the opposite reason: there the form gives no boundary
at all, here it gives one every four lines and the boundaries are **too frequent to be the cut**.

Four usable boundaries, in the order they are worth checking:

- **a change of figure** — the poem finishes with माँ and starts on पिताजी,
- **a change of speaker** — the poet stops speaking and a remembered voice begins
  (`और माँ ने कहा होगा…`), or a remembered voice ends and the poet resumes,
- **a change of tense or mode** — description becomes conjecture (`…रो पड़े होंगे`), conjecture
  becomes address (`हे सजीले हरे सावन`), address becomes instruction (`उन्हें कहना…`),
- **the return of a refrain-like line** — `पानी गिर रहा है` / `घर नजर में तिर रहा है` come back
  several times; each return re-opens the outer frame after an inner memory.

Rules — these are what a छंद-wise pipeline gets wrong:

- **Never split a स्तबक.** The rhymed pair is indivisible; a topic boundary falls only between
  स्तबक.
- **Never split a portrait.** If four स्तबक build one person, they are one topic. `concepts[]`
  carries the internal turns, exactly as a long पद or a long interview answer does.
- **A remembered speech is one topic with the speech intact.** `और माँ ने कहा होगा` and everything
  she is imagined to say belong together; cutting between the attribution and the words does to
  this form what splitting प्रश्न from उत्तर does to a साक्षात्कार.
- **The returning address gets its own small topic when it closes the poem.** If the opening
  address is repeated verbatim as the last स्तबक, that repetition **is** the ending and should be
  taught as one — a ring, not a leftover. (This is where the form differs from `mukt_chhand_kavita`,
  whose frame-return belongs with the final भाव-खंड: there the return is a line inside a खंड, here
  it is a whole स्तबक set apart on the page.)
- **The कवि-परिचय / परिचय-बॉक्स is `T1`.** In this form the biography is not background: the poem
  is unreadable without knowing where the speaker is and why.

## Emphasise

- **The gap between what is felt and what is sent.** This is the form's engine. The speaker aches
  and then dictates a cheerful message — `और कहना मस्त हूँ मैं`. Name the gap; do not resolve it
  into either grief or bravery.
- **Sound as weather.** `झरा-झर`, `हरा-हर`, `सर-सर`, `थर-थर` — ध्वन्यात्मक (onomatopoeic) doubling
  is how the rain gets into the metre. Read it aloud in class; it does not survive silent reading.
- **The doubled rhyme as a device, not a coincidence.** `गिर रहा है / घिर रहा है`,
  `मस्त हूँ मैं / अस्त हूँ मैं`. Two words a syllable apart, meaning opposite things — that is
  where the poem does its thinking.
- **लोकभाषा as the poet's choice.** `छिन`, `फलानी`, `भौजी`, `बड़`, `नद्दी`, `चुआ` are regional and
  deliberate; the poem is a letter home and wears the language of home. Keep them exactly and
  gloss them, never modernise (`reference/devanagari_verbatim.md`).
- **Who is in the room versus who is on the page.** Every family member is absent. The poem's
  people exist only as memory and conjecture (`होगा`, `होंगे`), and that conditional is the
  saddest word in the text. Point at it.
- **The natural world as messenger, not scenery.** The सावन is asked to carry a message; the हवा
  is asked to comfort. This is the दूत-काव्य tradition. Say so once, and connect it to what the
  child already knows about a message travelling.

## Avoid (hard gate)

- **Cutting one स्तबक per topic.** The most likely failure, and it wrecks every portrait.
- **Cutting mid-portrait or mid-speech**, or separating `कहा होगा` from what was said.
- **Turning the poem into a biography of the poet.** The jail is context, not the subject; the
  subject is a household seen from inside a cell.
- **Turning it into a patriotic lesson.** The chapter's own अभ्यास asks about घर, माँ, पिताजी and
  लगातार बरसता पानी — not about the freedom movement. The movement explains where he is sitting.
- **Reading the cheerful message literally.** `मस्त हूँ मैं` is not happiness; the poem says so
  four lines later.
- **Naming अलंकार that are not there**, and equally, missing the ones the poem is built on —
  ध्वन्यात्मक अनुप्रास, पुनरुक्ति, and the उपमा/रूपक pairs (`वज्र-भुज नवनीत-सा उर`).
- **Reflowing the verse into prose paragraphs** in `original_chunk`. Four lines are four lines.

## Priors

**Media** — one image per भाव-खंड. Two picture families alternate naturally and should be kept
distinct: the **cell** (present tense, cold light, bars, rain outside) and the **remembered house**
(courtyard, warm light, people). A frame that puts a remembered figure *inside* the cell is a
setting error unless the poem itself frames it as a vision. Where the poem addresses the sky, the
picture should look up.

**अभ्यास** — expect multiple-choice on what a line *implies* (`'और कहना मस्त हूँ मैं' किस बात की
ओर संकेत करता है?`), a `कविता की संरचना` question asking for the **चरण** of the emotional journey
(which is the profile's own cut, asked back), `कविता का सौंदर्य` on ध्वन्यात्मकता, `सृजन` asking
the child to write the letter one of the characters would have written, and व्याकरण on the
regional words. Where a topic prepares the letter-writing task, its explanation should already
have shown what that character is like.

**Language** — plain, spoken, regionally coloured खड़ी बोली. The words are easy; the feeling is
not. Gloss the दस-बारह genuinely regional or Sanskritic items (`चतुर्दिक्`, `नवनीत`, `लुनाई`,
`तृषा`) from the chapter's own शब्द-संपदा and leave the rest alone.

## Recall priors

One `remember` (what this खंड says, who it is about), one `understand` (what the image or the
conditional `होगा` means), one `analyze` or `evaluate` (why the poet says the opposite of what he
feels / why this figure is drawn with these two things at once).
