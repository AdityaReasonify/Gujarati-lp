# सूचनात्मक / सांस्कृतिक गद्य

## Diagnosis signals
Prose delivering **facts** — a dance, a festival, a craft, a place, a community's practice. Little
or no plot. Often names a real community, region or tradition. अभ्यास asks the child to recall
details, make a table or fact sheet, compare two practices, or find out about their own region.

## Lens
**तथ्य + परंपरा + जिज्ञासा** — a real practice, told so a child wants to know more.

## Guiding question
*यह परंपरा या तथ्य हमें अपने देश के बारे में क्या बताता है?*

## Explanation unit — **one तथ्य or one प्रथा**

One fact, custom or step per topic — the instrument, the costume, the occasion, the rules of the
game. Where the prose walks through a sequence (how a dance is performed, how a game is played),
each stage is a topic.

## Emphasise
- **The practice in its own terms.** Name it as its community names it — डाँडी, गोथा, दुईत,
  सत्रिया, बिहू — and explain what those words mean, rather than substituting a familiar
  equivalent.
- **Concrete detail.** Materials, movements, timing, who does what. This विधा is made of specifics
  and loses everything when generalised.
- **Curiosity, not distance.** The frame is "here is something in your country you may not know
  yet", never "here is how those people live".
- **Connection to the child's own world** — the game they play with what is at hand, the festival
  their own town keeps.
- **Comparison where the chapter invites it** (a folk game beside hockey), keeping both sides
  equal — the folk practice is not the lesser version.

## Avoid (hard gate)
- **Exoticising.** No "primitive", no "simple people", no framing a living practice as a curiosity
  from the past.
- **"Tribal people" or any generic label** where the chapter names a specific community. Say
  भील-भिलाला, say असमिया, say what the text says (`global_content_rules.md` §10).
- **Turning it into a Science or Social-Science lesson.** It is a Hindi chapter; teach the text
  and its language.
- **Adding facts the chapter does not carry** — no supplementing from general knowledge about the
  dance or the region.

## Priors

**Media** — one image per fact/practice, showing the practice **being done**, respectfully and
accurately: the real instrument, the real costume, the real setting. Where the chapter describes
materials (a bamboo ball, an L-shaped stick), show them as described.

**अभ्यास** — expect fact recall, tables and fact sheets, compare-and-contrast, vocabulary of the
practice, and a "find out about your own region" task. Where a topic prepares the table, its
explanation should name the categories the table will need.

**Language** — regional and domain vocabulary is the main load, and much of it will not be in a
standard dictionary. Gloss from the chapter's own explanation.

**Sensitivity** — a real community's living practice. Accurate naming, dignity, no ranking of
cultures.

## Recall priors
One `remember` (the fact), one `understand` (why it is done that way), one `apply` (a parallel in
the child's own place).
