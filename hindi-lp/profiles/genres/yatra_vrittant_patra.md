# यात्रा-वृत्तांत / पत्र

## Diagnosis signals

First-person prose that **moves through places in order**. A salutation opens it and a sign-off
closes it — `श्रीमान कविवचन सुधा संपादक महामहिम मित्रवरेषु!` … `अगले नंबरों में लिखूँगा।` — so it is
addressed to a named reader, not to the world. The tense is the present or the immediate past:
the writer is **there now**, or has just been.

Between the salutation and the sign-off the text advances by **location**: the approach, the river,
the ghat, the people met, the departure. Description dominates; there is no plot and no turn.

**Tested before `soochnatmak_sanskritik` and `sansmaran`.** A सूचनात्मक piece is organised by
subject and could be reordered; a यात्रा-वृत्तांत could not, because the order is the journey. A
संस्मरण looks back from a distance; this is written from inside the trip, often to meet a deadline.

## Lens

**पड़ाव + दृष्टि + संबोधन** — where the writer is, what they notice there, and the person they are
telling.

## Guiding question

*लेखक यहाँ रुका क्यों, और उसने वही क्यों देखा जो देखा?*

## Explanation unit — **one पड़ाव**

A **पड़ाव** is one stop on the journey — a place, or a stretch of the road that the writer treats
as one. The boundary is a **change of location or of what is in front of the writer**, not a
paragraph break and not a change of subject.

- **The salutation is part of the first पड़ाव**, not a separate topic. It is the letter announcing
  itself, and it sets the voice for everything after.
- **The sign-off closes the last पड़ाव.** `इसका वृत्तांत अगले नंबरों में लिखूँगा` is the journey
  admitting it is not finished — teach that, do not drop it.
- **A digression stays inside its पड़ाव.** These writers wander: a description of trees becomes a
  reflection on generosity, then returns to the road. The reflection happened at that spot and
  belongs to it. Use `concepts[]` for the internal turns.
- **Do not reorder.** Logical order and printed order are the same here, and `learning_plan_logical`
  should say so.

## Emphasise

- **The eye, not the encyclopaedia.** What the writer noticed is the content. `बया` nests hanging
  twenty and thirty to a branch, on a thorn tree of all trees — that is observation, and the
  teachable thing is the noticing, not ornithology.
- **The addressee shapes the writing.** Written to a magazine editor, for readers, in instalments.
  That is why it is vivid and why it stops where it stops.
- **Simile as the traveller's tool.** Trees standing `मानो एक पैर से खड़े तपस्या करते हैं`;
  creepers spreading like `सज्जनों के शुभ मनोरथ`. Name the उपमा/उत्प्रेक्षा where it is genuinely
  there, and say what the comparison lets the reader see.
- **Old Hindi as an object of study.** Where the prose is a century or more old, its shape *is*
  a topic: longer sentences, Sanskrit compounds, words in senses now lost. Point at one such word
  per पड़ाव and let the child feel the distance. Never modernise `original_chunk`
  (`reference/devanagari_verbatim.md`).
- **Place with respect.** Pilgrimage towns, ghats, the people who work them. Describe the practice
  as the text describes it; the profile shares `soochnatmak_sanskritik`'s standard here.

## Avoid (hard gate)

- **Cutting by topic instead of by पड़ाव** — scattering a journey that moves in order.
- **Turning it into a travel guide or a geography lesson.** Distances, populations and history the
  text does not give are not to be supplied (`reference/no_hallucination_policy.md`).
- **Dropping the salutation and the sign-off**, or treating them as formatting.
- **Modernising or transliterating the old Hindi.**
- **Exoticising the pilgrims, the पंडे, or the ritual** — or, equally, explaining faith away.
- **Reading the writer's period assumptions as the pack's voice.** Report what the text says;
  where a phrase would land badly today, handle it in `08_sensitivity.json`, not by silent edit.

## Priors

**Media** — one image per पड़ाव, in journey order, showing the place as the text describes it.
Photographs of the real location may already exist in the chapter's art (हरिद्वार's हर की पैड़ी
is a photograph in the printed book); do not mix a photograph and an illustration inside one
composite. A map is a legitimate single image for the chapter if the text names its stops.

**अभ्यास** — expect comprehension per पड़ाव, `सोच-विचार के लिए` on why the writer valued travel,
`भाषा की बात` on the old vocabulary, `आपकी बात` inviting the child's own journey, and often a
`झरोखे से` carrying a second extract by the same writer — which is another पड़ाव and another
topic.

**Language** — period prose, Sanskritised, long-breathed. Gloss heavily and precisely; a word in a
lost sense (`अर्थी`, `वल्ली`, `कल्लोल`) is worse than an unknown word, because the child will
confidently read it wrong.

## Recall priors

One `remember` (where the writer is, what is there), one `understand` (why it struck them),
one `analyze` or `apply` (what the comparison shows / describe a place you know this way).
