# डायरी

## Diagnosis signals

A **date stands as a heading**, and the text under it begins by naming that date again —
*26 जनवरी : आज का दिन तो अमर दिन है।* First person. Past tense for what happened, present for the
writing itself.

Inside the entry the order is **the clock**, not the plot: भोर छह बजे → ग्यारह बजे → दो-तीन बजे →
ठीक चार बजकर दस मिनट. Times are given exactly, often to the minute, because the writer is
recording rather than composing.

The two decisive signals:

- **The writer does not know how it ends.** Nothing is foreshadowed, nothing is arranged. Events
  arrive in the order they arrived.
- **The writer marks their own evidence.** *यह सब तो अपने सुनी हुई लिख रहे हैं* … *यह अपनी आँख से
  देखा* — heard, versus seen. A diarist separates the two because they are writing for themselves
  and accuracy is the only reason to write at all.

Nearby profiles and why they are wrong here:

- **`sansmaran`** is memoir: written long after, shaped by knowing the outcome, and its unit is
  one जीवन-चरण. A diary is written the same night. Applying the संस्मरण lens imports hindsight the
  text does not have and is the single likeliest way to lose this form.
- **`soochnatmak_sanskritik`** also reports facts — but a diary's facts are one person's, from one
  vantage point, with the gaps that implies.

## Lens

**दिन + दृष्टि + दस्तावेज़** — one day, seen from one place, written down as it happened.

## Guiding question

*जो आदमी नहीं जानता कि दिन कैसे ख़त्म होगा, वह उसे कैसे लिखता है?*

## Explanation unit — **one प्रसंग of the day, in clock order**

A प्रसंग is a stretch of the entry held together by **one time and one place** — the morning
decoration of the city; the school's flag-raising at eleven; the meeting at the मैदान; the लाठीचार्ज.
When the clock moves and the place moves, the topic ends.

Three consequences:

- **Cut by the clock, not by theme.** Gathering every mention of the police into one topic is the
  natural instinct and it is wrong: it re-composes the day into an essay, which is precisely what
  the writer did not do. The diary's argument *is* its sequence.
- **A single dated entry is still many topics.** "एक पन्ना" describes the page, not the unit. One
  entry can hold a whole day.
- **Where two things happen at once, the text says so** — *इधर यह हालत हो रही थी कि उधर …*. Keep
  them in one topic; the simultaneity is the writer telling you they could not be in both places.

## Emphasise

- **Seen versus heard.** Wherever the diarist marks the difference, teach it. It is the most
  valuable transferable idea in the form and it is a lesson about evidence, not about history.
- **Numbers are the voice.** दो हज़ार रुपया, छह बजे, चार बजकर चौबीस मिनट, ग्यारह बजे. A diarist
  counts. Keep the figures in the explanation rather than rounding them away — precision *is* the
  characterisation.
- **Real names are real people.** सुभाष बाबू, अविनाश बाबू, ज्योतिर्मय गांगुली, क्षितीश चटर्जी,
  जानकीदेवी, मदालसा. Name them as the text does. Do not promote a passing name to a main character,
  and do not quietly drop the women's names — a diary that lists who was there is testimony about
  who was there.
- **The writer's own position.** `अपने` used for oneself, the arrangements the writer was
  personally responsible for, the admission that his own share last year was ordinary. The 'मैं'
  of a diary is modest and busy, not heroic.
- **The day's shape.** Preparation → the crowd gathering → the announced hour → what actually
  happened at that hour. The gap between *चार बजकर चौबीस मिनट पर झंडा फहराया जाएगा* and what the
  clock actually delivered is the entry's spine.

## Avoid (hard gate)

- **Reading with hindsight.** No "और आगे चलकर देश आज़ाद हुआ", no framing the day as a step toward a
  known outcome. The writer did not have that and neither does the topic.
- **Re-ordering the day** into themes, causes, or a tidy beginning-middle-end.
- **Turning the diarist into a hero, or the crowd into a mass.** Individuals are named; keep them
  individual.
- **Converting the entry into a history lesson.** Dates and movements may be glossed briefly where
  the text names them, but the topic teaches the page, not the syllabus around it.
- **Softening or dwelling on the violence.** Where the text records injury — a split head, blood,
  lathis — report it as the diarist did: plainly, once, without lingering and without removing it.
  Follow `reference/global_content_rules.md`; the entry's own restraint is the model.
- **Inventing what the writer could not have known** — what happened at the other end of the city,
  what an official was thinking, how many were hurt in total.

## Priors

**Media** — one image per प्रसंग, showing **that hour in that place**: the decorated बड़ा बाज़ार in
the morning, the school hall at eleven, the मैदान filling at three. A collage is a natural fit
because the clock supplies the panel order for free. Avoid a single montage of "the freedom
struggle"; the form's whole claim is that this is one day, not an era.

**अभ्यास** — expect comprehension per प्रसंग, questions on the writer's attitude and on the
difference between his account and a newspaper's, and very often a task asking the child to
**keep their own diary** or to write the day from another person's vantage. Where a topic prepares
that, its explanation should name what makes a diary usable later: the time, the place, what was
seen rather than heard.

**Language** — plain reporting prose with period and regional idiom (`अपने` for oneself, `प्रबंध`,
`तलब`, `कानून भंग`). Gloss the idiom; do not modernise it. Sentences are short and declarative —
the register a child can actually imitate, which is why the "write your own" task is set.

## Recall priors

One `remember` (what happened at that hour), one `understand` (why the writer recorded that
detail), one `analyze` or `evaluate` (seen or heard — how do you know; what would a different
witness have written).
