# कुंडलिया

## Diagnosis signals

Six lines. The first two are a **दोहा**, the next four a **रोला** — and they are locked together
by two rules you can see on the page:

1. The दोहा's last foot **is** the रोला's first foot, repeated word for word.
2. The last word (or phrase) of line 6 **is** the first word of line 1.

The poem therefore closes back onto itself — that is what *कुंडलिया* means (कुंडल = a ring).
A कवि-छाप (`कह गिरिधर कविराय`) usually sits in line 5. Theme is लोकनीति — practical, everyday
wisdom, often already circulating as a proverb.

**The tell:** if you can see the same phrase ending line 2 and beginning line 3, and the poem's
first and last words match, it is a कुंडलिया and nothing else.

## Lens

**चक्र + नीति + दृष्टांत** — the ring, the practical lesson, and the everyday picture that
carries it.

## Guiding question

*कविता जिस शब्द से शुरू हुई, वहीं लौटकर क्यों ख़त्म होती है?*

## Explanation unit — **one कुंडलिया** (all six lines)

**Hard gate. A कुंडलिया is never split.** Not into दोहा + रोला, not into three couplets, not into
"the first half and the second half". Cutting it anywhere destroys the two joins that define the
form, and both joins are the teaching point.

This is the opposite of `niti_doha.md`, and the distinction matters: there, one दोहा is a
complete unit and merging two is the error. Here the दोहा is only the **opening** of a longer
unit, and splitting it off is the error. Diagnose the form before choosing the unit.

If a chapter has two कुंडलिया, that is two topics. Not four, not six.

## Emphasise

- **The two joins, named and shown.** Have the child point at the repeated foot between line 2
  and line 3, and at the first/last word match. Do not merely assert that the poem is circular —
  make them find it.
- **What the repetition does to the meaning.** The दोहा states the trouble; the रोला picks up its
  own last phrase and unfolds the consequences. The repeat is a hinge, not filler.
- **The नीति in the child's own life.** These are proverbs people still say. Ask where they have
  heard one.
- **The कवि-छाप** — `कह गिरिधर कविराय` is the poet signing inside the poem, a convention worth
  naming once.
- **ब्रजभाषा forms as they stand**: `करै`, `पावै`, `भावै`, `देइ`, `लेइ`, `जिय`, `माहिं`. Gloss
  them; never rewrite them into खड़ी बोली.

## Avoid (hard gate)

- **Splitting the कुंडलिया.** The single most damaging error available here.
- **Teaching the दोहा and ignoring the रोला** — the दोहा is the famous half and the easy half,
  which is exactly why it gets taught alone.
- **Reducing the poem to its proverb.** *"बिना बिचारे जो करै सो पाछे पछिताय"* is quotable, but the
  poem's work is the four lines that show what the regret actually feels like — `चित्त में चैन न
  पावै`, `खान पान सन्मान … मनहिं न भावै`.
- **A moral lecture.** The नीति is demonstrated; it does not need announcing again at the end.
- **Correcting the ब्रज.** `पछिताय` is not a misspelling of `पछताना`.

## Priors

**Media** — one image per कुंडलिया. A composite suits this form unusually well: panel १ the
दोहा's situation, panel २ the consequence the रोला unfolds, panel ३ the return. If panels are
used, the last should visually echo the first — the picture can carry the ring that the words do.

**अभ्यास** — expect भाव-स्पष्ट करो per कुंडलिया, proverb-matching, "have you heard this said?",
and often a task asking the child to spot the repeated words. Where an exercise asks for the
repetition, the topic's explanation must already have named it.

**Language** — ब्रजभाषा with 18th-century लोक vocabulary. The words a class-7 child stalls on are
the verb endings, not the nouns. Gloss `करै/पावै/भावै` as a set once rather than one at a time.

## Recall priors

One `remember` (what the कुंडलिया advises), one `understand` (what the रोला adds to the दोहा),
one `analyze` (find the two joins / why the poem returns to its first word).
