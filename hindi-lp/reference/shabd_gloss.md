# शब्द-गloss — which words to explain, and how

Drives `key_terms`, the inline glossing inside `explanation`, `concept_bullets`, and the module's
`difficult_words`.

## Which words earn a gloss

Gloss a word when a class-6 child reading it **stops**. In Hindi that happens for four reasons,
and each needs a different kind of gloss:

| Kind | Why it stops the child | Gloss with |
|---|---|---|
| **तत्सम** — unchanged Sanskrit (`पुण्य`, `सुयश`, `पुनीत`, `निराली`) | formal register, rarely spoken at home | the everyday तद्भव or spoken equivalent |
| **तद्भव / देशज** in an old or regional form (`अमराइयाँ`, `छहर`, `माखन`, `नहिं`) | familiar root, unfamiliar shape | the current खड़ी बोली form |
| **उर्दू-मूल** (`ज़िंदगी`, `मुक़ाबला`, `शर्मिंदा`) | usually known, sometimes not | plain Hindi, and keep the नुक़्ता |
| **काव्य-लाइसेंस** (`यमुन` for `यमुना`, `मातुभूमि`) | looks like a spelling error | say it is लय, not a mistake |

Lead with words the child will **meet again** — `साधना`, `लगन`, `कृतज्ञता`, `विनम्रता`,
`परंपरा` — over one-off ornamental words. A word that appears once in one poem and nowhere else in
the child's reading life is a lower priority than one that will return in every essay.

**Skip** everyday words. Glossing `पेड़` or `पानी` wastes the child's attention and signals that
you are filling a field.

## How to write the gloss

- **Student-friendly and concrete, never dictionary-style.**
  `सिंधु — समुद्र; यहाँ हिंद महासागर के लिए आया है` — good.
  `सिंधु — जलराशि का विस्तीर्ण भाग` — useless to an eleven-year-old.
- **Gloss at the point of use** inside `explanation`, the moment the word appears — not in a list
  the child must scroll back to.
- `key_terms` entries use the form `शब्द — अर्थ`, matching the class-6 v1 plans.
- `concept_bullets` / `important_points` lead with the keyword, e.g.
  `कर्मभूमि — वह भूमि जहाँ मेहनत और कर्तव्य निभाया जाता है`.
- For a **काव्य-लाइसेंस**, say what happened and why:
  `'यमुन' यहाँ 'यमुना' के लिए आया है — कवि ने लय बनाए रखने के लिए शब्द थोड़ा बदला है।`

## The trap

A Hindi textbook often prints its own शब्दार्थ list. Use it as evidence of what the book thinks is
hard — but **do not copy it in place of glossing at point of use**, and do not assume it is
complete. The child's stopping point, not the printed list, decides.
