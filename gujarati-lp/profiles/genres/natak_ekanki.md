## Diagnosis signals

**Speaker labels down the left, each name governing every line until the next name**, with
**રંગસૂચના (stage directions) in round brackets** both between and inside speeches — `(હસીને)`,
`(ગંભીર બનીને)`, `(હર્ષથી)` (std-7 ch-14); `(અધવચથી અટકાવી)`, `(ખડખડાટ હસતાં)`, `(પોક મૂકે છે.)`
(std-9 ch-9). In the std 6–8 books the label is printed in colour; in std 9–10 it is plain
(`ડૉક્ટર :`). No narrator, no descriptive passage that is not a direction.

## Lens

**સંવાદ + રંગસૂચના + વળાંક** — what is said, what the stage is told to do, and the turn the two produce
between them.

## Guiding question

*જે મંચ પર બતાવી ન શકાય, તે વાત સંવાદ અને રંગસૂચના દ્વારા પ્રેક્ષક સુધી કેવી રીતે પહોંચે છે ?*

A second form, where the chapter turns on motive rather than on staging:
*આ દૃશ્યમાં કોને શું જોઈએ છે, અને કઈ વાત તેને બદલી નાખે છે ?*

Each chapter derives its own from its own text; these are shapes, not fill-ins.

## Explanation unit — **one stage-beat** (દૃશ્ય-પ્રસંગ / સંવાદ-ખંડ)

A beat is a stretch of the એકાંકી held together by one situation, and **it ends when the stage
changes.** The રંગસૂચના are therefore the cut marks — that is what makes the unit decidable, and it
matters because three of the four measured GSEB plays (std-8, std-9, std-10) carry **no scene
numbering at all**; only std-7 ch-14 prints `(પ્રવેશ પહેલો)` / `(પ્રવેશ બીજો)`, and even there each
પ્રવેશ holds several beats. Reading such a chapter as one undivided lump, or cutting it by page, both
destroy the shape.

In practice the boundary is almost always one of:

- **an entrance or an exit** — ઉદો walks in with the news that રાણો મેવાડ છોડી જાય છે; ભામાશા arrives
  in પ્રવેશ બીજો. Someone new is on stage and what can be said changes;
- **a sound or an object arriving** — a treasure is set down, a letter is read out, a bell rings;
- **a change in what a character knows** — a name is heard, a ગેરસમજ is realised (the whole engine of
  std-9 ch-9 પારખું);
- **a change in what a character wants** — ભામાશા refuses જગડુ's war-profiteering deal and the scene's
  business is over; patience turns to anger, hospitality to a plea.

Four rules follow, and they are the ones a generic pipeline gets wrong:

- **The header is its own topic.** The `પાત્રો` list with its one-phrase descriptions, the
  `(સ્થળ : … સમય : …)` line, the date in ઝબક જ્યોત's opening paragraph, the opening full-paragraph
  direction — these go together in one topic. This is not front matter to be skipped: it is the
  audience's entire orientation delivered in a few lines, and reading it is a skill. `સમય : સવારના દસ`
  is information — a waiting-room at ten in the morning with no patients is the joke before anyone
  speaks. `કેતકી : એક આઠ વર્ષની કન્યા.` tells the child whose eyes the play is seen through.
- **Never cut mid-exchange, and never separate a speech from the direction that governs it.** If A
  asks and B answers, that pair is inside one beat; `(તલવાર ખેંચી) જય એકલિંગજી !` is one thing.
- **A long expository exchange stays ONE topic.** ભામાશા and જગડુ arguing across a page about a
  bargain neither will close is one beat, because nothing on the stage changes. Use the `concepts[]`
  layer for its internal turns — what is being offered, why it is refused, what that refusal reveals —
  exactly as a long પદ or a long interview answer is handled. **Do not cut it into five topics because
  five facts arrived.** A play does not get more topics for being talkative.
- **A single line can be its own topic** when a direction makes it the turn. The line delivered as a
  character walks out, or the one the closing direction lands on, is a beat by itself.

Do **not** cut by page, by speech count, or by character. Cut by what changes on the stage.

## Emphasise

- **રંગસૂચના are text, not formatting and not stage furniture.** They carry the plot: `(હર્ષથી)`
  carries the relief, `(અધવચથી અટકાવી)` is an interruption the words alone do not show,
  `(સહુ મેવાડનો જય ગજાવે છે. પડદો પડે છે.)` is the ending itself. Keep them inside `original_chunk`
  exactly as printed — brackets and all — and say in the explanation what the direction tells the
  audience that the dialogue does not. **A plan that keeps only the spoken lines has deleted half the
  play.**
- **The exposition problem.** An એકાંકી has no narrator, so everything must be spoken by someone who
  has a reason to speak it. Show the child the device: a મુનીમ asks so that the audience can be told
  what the પેઢી is worth; a patient's question exists so the doctor can boast. Naming this is the
  single most transferable thing in the form.
- **Subtext — what is said versus what is meant.** ભામાશા's courtesy to a man he is refusing;
  પારખું's doctor claiming a practice he does not have. Name the gap; do not flatten it to politeness.
- **Dramatic irony — what the audience knows and a character does not.** હળવું એકાંકી lives on it:
  the reader sees the ગેરસમજ a beat before the character does. Do not spoil it early; teach it at the
  beat where it lands.
- **The closing line.** These endings are short and often **return an earlier phrase with its meaning
  changed**. Where a phrase comes back, put the two occurrences side by side and ask what changed
  between them — the words did not.
- **The stage as a real constraint.** One room, one દીવાનખાનું, one દવાખાનું, real time. Everything
  must happen in front of the audience — that is why news has to be carried in by a person and a
  decision has to be announced aloud. The unity is what lets a few pages hold a war.
- **Register belongs to the character, and this form is idiom-dense.** std-7 ch-14's
  rhetorical-patriotic speech runs on idiom — ઊગતા સૂરજને પૂજો, પાપનું પોટલું બાંધવું, ધૂળ ચાટતા થઈ જશે,
  આંગળીને વેઢે હિસાબ ગણવો; std-9 ch-9 mixes બુઝુર્ગ and લિબાસ with **Roman-script English lines inside
  the speeches** ("Hard facts.", "I mean my compounder."). Gloss the register; never neutralise it, and
  never delete the English — it is printed text, not an extraction defect.
- **લય — આરોહ-અવરોહ.** std-7 ch-14's intro box instructs "સંવાદોનાં વાક્યોનો લય (આરોહ-અવરોહ) સ્પષ્ટ
  કરવો." Where a line rises or drops, say so; that is what the child is being asked to hear.
- **Comic timing where it exists.** A misunderstanding, an entrance at the worst moment, a repeated
  phrase coming back. Say why it is funny; a joke explained flatly stops being one.
- **Performability.** The child will be asked to act this. Where a line is hard to say aloud, or a
  direction hard to stage, point at it.

## Avoid (hard gate)

Each is a machine-checkable statement. Agent 7 instantiates them per topic, Agent 13 blocks on the
hard ones; the structural items marked **(A4)** are checked at Agent 4 before ids freeze.

- **No supplied narrator.** No `explanation`, `real_life_example`, `brief_summary`, `summary` or
  `detailed_summary` may contain a narrator formula — `લેખક કહે છે કે`, `પછી ભામાશાએ વિચાર્યું`,
  `તેને લાગ્યું કે` — or any interior state that is neither spoken in a line nor printed in a
  રંગસૂચના inside that topic's `original_chunk`.
- **No dropped or paraphrased રંગસૂચના.** Every parenthesised direction printed inside the beat
  appears character-for-character in `original_chunk`; the count of `(` in `original_chunk` is not less
  than the count on the rendered page for that stretch. A direction rewritten as narration ("પછી એક
  સૈનિક આવ્યો") is a hard fail. This is the commonest failure, and the one that turns the form into
  prose.
- **No reassigned speech.** Every speaker label printed in the beat appears in `original_chunk`, in
  printed order, attached to the same words as on the page. Names are the only cue the reader has; one
  error corrupts the whole scene.
- **The header is never dropped and never absorbed.** Exactly one topic carries the `પાત્રો` block,
  the opening direction and any `સ્થળ`/`સમય`/date line; no other topic's `original_chunk` contains
  them, and no chapter with a printed header lacks such a topic. **(A4)**
- **No cut through a governed pair.** No topic's `original_chunk` begins with a parenthesised
  direction whose speech sits in the previous topic, and none ends with a speech whose governing
  direction sits in the next; no topic boundary falls between a question and the answer that follows
  it. **(A4)**
- **No topic inflation.** The chapter's topic count does not exceed the number of stage changes
  recorded in `structure_inventory` (entrances, exits, arrivals, printed `પ્રવેશ`/`દૃશ્ય` headers,
  direction-marked turns) plus one for the header. A long single-situation exchange split into several
  topics is a hard fail; its internal turns belong in `concepts[]`. **(A4)**
- **No tacked-on બોધ.** No topic's authored prose ends on `બોધ`, `સંદેશ`, `શિખામણ`, `ઉપદેશ` or
  `આપણે શીખવું જોઈએ`. A play argues by putting two people in a room; it resolves a situation, it does
  not issue a moral.
- **Nothing offstage invented.** Every statement in `explanation` and the summaries is traceable to a
  spoken line or a printed direction in this chapter. No resolution supplied for an ending the
  playwright left open, no biography of a character beyond what the text states.
- **The printed text is never "corrected."** A cast list that omits a speaker who appears, a name
  spelled two ways on one page, a duplicated block number, Roman-script English inside a speech, an
  elision (`'ભલા'દમી'`) or an interruption's ત્રણ ટપકાં — all stay exactly as printed. Record the
  oddity in `07_pitfalls.json`; keep the verbatim per `reference/gujarati_verbatim.md`.
- **સ્વાધ્યાય is never a topic — including embedded scripts.** std-8 ch-12's exercise block 3 prints a
  whole second play-excerpt (`સ્થળ : પાટણ`, રાજા વિશળદેવ's દરબાર, જગડુશા) as comprehension material.
  It looks like text and is not: it is exercise apparatus, it appears in `exercise_inventory`, and
  Agent 10 owns it. No topic's `original_chunk` may contain any line printed inside a સ્વાધ્યાય block.

## Priors

**Media** — one image per beat, composed as **the stage at that moment**: who is on it, where they
stand, who is entering, who is turned away, what the direction just did. Prefer the moment a direction
describes over a portrait of a speaker. The set stays consistent across the chapter's images because
it is one room — a frame showing a different room is a wrong match no matter how well the action fits.
A collage suits this form unusually well, since entrances and exits give the panels their own order
(the room before · the arrival · the two alone). **But there is no Gujarati frame pool**: Agent 9's
reuse step is dormant, every scene gets `image_url: ""` with an authored, self-contained
`generation_prompt`, and `reuse_report` still emits with `reused: 0` and `rejected: []`. The narrator
bar carries one exact line of Gujarati in Gujarati script; `negative_prompt` includes
`Devanagari script labels`.

**સ્વાધ્યાય** — inventory the printed headings per chapter (§3.4); never assume this list. What was

Where a topic prepares the અભિનય task, its explanation should name what an actor would need from the
line: what the character wants in that beat, who is being addressed, at what volume, moving where —
and, per std-7's own instruction, where the line rises and where it falls. Where a topic prepares
પાત્રાલેખન, it should name the trait **and the line that shows it**.

**Language** — everyday-to-formal spoken Gujarati, split by character rather than by chapter. The
printed word apparatus differs by standard and is **evidence of what the book thinks is hard, never
the source of the gloss** (`reference/shabd_gloss.md`): std-7 ch-14 folds about forty entries
*including its idioms* into a single શબ્દાર્થ box with no separate રૂઢિપ્રયોગ block and **no grammar
box**; std-8 ch-12 prints a ten-idiom રૂઢિપ્રયોગ pre-block; std-9 ch-9 splits its શબ્દ-સમજૂતી into
સમાનાર્થી / વિરુદ્ધાર્થી / રૂઢિપ્રયોગ / કહેવત. Period and register items need glossing at point of
first use — પટાવત, સામંત, પેઢી, મુનીમ, દલાલી, જુહાર (std-7); લિબાસ, બુઝુર્ગ, રાસ્કલ (std-9). The L2
bar sits lower than a first-language pack's: gloss more, not less. Punctuation exactly as printed —
`.` as પૂર્ણવિરામ, never the Devanagari daṇḍa (U+0964) — along with ત્રણ ટપકાં, લોપચિહ્ન and the absence of અવતરણચિહ્નો around
ઉક્તિ, per `reference/gujarati_verbatim.md`.

**Sensitivity** — flag and guide, never censor (Agent 8). std-7 ch-14 stages the Mewar–Mughal conflict
(રાણા પ્રતાપ, અકબર, હલ્દીઘાટી) in a patriotic register with દુશ્મન language in its idiom box: teach the
play's own lens — દાન and વતનપ્રેમ, a person against an injustice — and never let it become a communal
frame. std-8 ch-12 turns on an unmarried ફોઈ inside a joint family, and the book itself prints a
`સંયુક્ત કુટુંબ અને વિભક્ત કુટુંબના લાભાલાભ` discussion: guide the conversation, never judge a character
or a family form. std-9 ch-9's comedy runs on a man's unemployment and his boasting — teach the
irony, not contempt for the character.

## Recall priors

Per topic, two to three questions, `bloom_level` lowercase, each with a real model answer drawn from
this beat's `original_chunk` and its directions:

- one **remember** — who is on stage in this beat, who speaks the line, what the direction does;
- one **understand** — why that thing had to be spoken aloud at all (the play has no narrator to say
  it), or what the direction tells the audience that the words do not;
- one from **analyze / apply / evaluate** — what changed between the two occurrences of a returning
  phrase; what the audience knows that the character does not; how you would speak this line and where
  you would move.

The header topic gets real questions too, not filler: who the play's people are, where and when it
stands, and what that room or that hour has already told us before anyone speaks.
