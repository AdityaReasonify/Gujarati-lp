## Diagnosis signals

Prose with named પાત્રો, a chain of ઘટના, dialogue, and a વળાંક. The theme is carried by what
happens, not by a stated sentence. Past-tense narration; dialogue printed inside `''…''` inside the
paragraphs (std-6 ch 2, std-7 ch 8, ch 10, ch 12) or, in court and classroom scenes, as short
labelled turns inside otherwise narrative prose (`રાજાભોજ :`, `કવિ :` — std-7 ch 2).

**The book usually names its own sub-form in the blue intro box**, and that label is the strongest
single signal — but it is evidence, not a verdict, and it is often broad ("આ વાર્તામાં…"):

- "આ એક રસપ્રદ રમૂજકથા છે…" (std-6 ch 2) · "આ એક ચાતુર્યકથા છે." (std-7 ch 2)
- "આ એક વીરરસભરી પૌરાણિક કથા છે." (std-7 ch 3) · "…આ એક પ્રેરક અને પૌરાણિક કથા છે" (std-8 ch 2)
- "શૌર્ય અને સ્વાર્પણની લોકકથાઓ… તેમાંની જ આ એક કથા છે." (std-7 ch 8)
- "માણસ અને પ્રાણી વચ્ચે બંધાતા સ્નેહસંબંધની આ વાર્તા છે." (std-7 ch 10)
- "આ એક પૌરાણિક વાર્તા છે, જેમાં આદર્શ રાજ્ય કેવું હોય એનું સરસ દૃષ્ટાંત આપવામાં આવ્યું છે." (std-7 P3)
- "લઘુકથા સાહિત્યપ્રકારમાં લાઘવ અને અંતે આવતી ચોટનું અત્યંત મહત્ત્વ છે." (std-9 ch 11)

**Signal C — the સ્વાધ્યાય tells (measured, std 6–8).** Three block types route here almost by
themselves, because each of them only makes sense if the chapter has a plot:

- an event-ordering block — "નીચેની ઘટનાઓને પાઠના ક્રમમાં ગોઠવીને ફરીથી લખો." (std-6 ch 2),
  "નીચેનાં વાક્યોને યોગ્ય ઘટનાક્રમમાં ગોઠવો." (std-7 ch 3), "નીચેનાં વાક્યોને પાઠના ઘટનાક્રમ મુજબ
  ગોઠવો." (std-6 ch 13, std-7 ch 7, ch 13);
- a speaker-attribution block — "નીચેનાં વાક્યો કોણ બોલ્યું હશે એ લખો." (std-7 ch 2),
  "આપેલાં વાક્યો વાંચો, વિચારો અને તે કોણ બોલે છે તે લખો." (std-7 ch 10), std-8 ch 4 blk 7;
- a counterfactual inside વાતચીત — "સાદ ન ઓળખ્યો હોત તો ?" (std-7 ch 8), "બે રૂપિયા પાછા ન આપ્યા
  હોત તો ?" (std-7 ch 12), "પોતાં ન મૂક્યાં હોત તો ?" (std-7 ch 10), "ખરેખર હનુમાનજી સૂર્યને ગળી
  ગયા હોત તો શું થાત ?" (std-6 ch 13) — plus motive questions in the written block
  ("ઝળઝળિયાં કેમ આવી ગયાં હશે ?", std-7 ch 8).

At std 9–10 the apparatus is fixed and carries no such tell: the four-tier ladder (✓-MCQ →
એક-એક વાક્યમાં → બે-ત્રણ વાક્યમાં → સવિસ્તર/સવિસ્તાર) runs under every સ્વરૂપ alike. There the
diagnosis rests on the printed form and the intro box — and at std 9 sometimes on the MCQ itself,
which names the સાહિત્યપ્રકાર as its answer (std-9 ch 4's answer is **નવલકથાખંડ**).

## Lens

**ઘટના + પાત્ર + વળાંક**

## Guiding question

*પાત્રની કઈ પસંદગી વાર્તાને વળાંક આપે છે?*

The wording above is a **shape**. Each chapter derives its own from its own text and Agent 1 writes
it into `01_meta.json` — for std-7 ch 8 it is about a voice recognised across an enmity, for std-7
ch 12 about honesty a child could not afford. A guiding question that would fit any story in the
book has not been derived.

## Explanation unit — **one ઘટના**

One plot beat per topic: a coherent event with its own small arc. Typical shape across the chapter:
**પરિચય → ગૂંચ → સંઘર્ષ → વળાંક → પરિણામ**. Follow the story's turns, not paragraph counts.

- A long descriptive passage that does not move the story **joins** the beat it belongs to.
- A single sentence that turns everything **is** its own topic (std-7 ch 3's closing
  "ચક્રવ્યૂહ તૂટ્યો પણ આપણો ભડવીર ખૂટ્યો !" carries the whole chapter's meaning).
- Repetition with escalation is a chain of beats, not one beat: in std-7 P3 each of the four
  "ઉત્તમ પુરુષો" who pockets a મોતી is a beat of the same test.
- The event list printed in a સ્વાધ્યાય ordering block is **evidence for the arc, never the cut** —
  five printed items do not mean five topics, and those items are Agent 10's material.
- **Frame stories** (std-6 ch 13): the classroom frame and the embedded કથા are different ઘટના and
  different topics. The sentence that hands over to the teller opens the embedded topic; the
  sentence that returns to the frame opens the closing frame topic. `topic_category` marks the
  handover as `transition`.
- **Verse printed inside a prose chapter**: couplets quoted inside a scene (the લગ્નગીત snippets in
  std-10 ch 2) stay verbatim inside that scene's topic. A complete composition printed at the end
  with its own speaker labels (std-6 ch 11's family-made poem) is its own topic — its ઘટના is the
  family composing it, and it is not re-cut કડી-wise.
- Sub-forms do not change the unit. A લોકકથા is cut by ઘટના exactly as a ટૂંકીવાર્તા is; the
  oral register changes the Language prior, not the boundary.

Cutting rules 1–5 of `reference/explanation_unit_map.md` apply unchanged: exact verbatim first, a
pre-topic hook belongs to the NEXT topic, single-theme topics, સ્વાધ્યાય is never a topic.

## નવલકથાખંડ અને લઘુકથા — the excerpt rule

A **નવલકથાખંડ** is a slice of a longer book: std-9 ch 4 comes from ધ્રુવ ભટ્ટની 'અકૂપાર' and std-10
ch 3 from the હાસ્યનવલકથા 'ભદ્રંભદ્ર'. The printed excerpt may hold an exposition and a rising
action with no resolution at all, or a resolution whose setup lives in a chapter the child will
never see.

**Cut on the ઘટના that IS present. Never invent a resolution.** Concretely:

- No topic may narrate an event that is not in `original_chunk`, including "what happened next".
- `detailed_summary`, `explanation` and recall answers describe the excerpt's arc, not the novel's.
  Where the excerpt genuinely ends unresolved, the teaching says so plainly — an open ending is a
  fact about the text, not a gap to fill.
- The source line printed with the excerpt ("…'અકૂપાર' નવલકથામાંથી") is attribution: it belongs
  inside the LAST topic's `original_chunk` per `reference/gujarati_verbatim.md`, and it is not a
  licence to summarise the parent novel.

A **લઘુકથા** is complete but tiny — std-9 ch 11 વારસાગત and std-10 ch 17 ટિફિન each run about a
page, and the whole form lives on "લાઘવ અને અંતે આવતી ચોટ". Two or three topics for the whole
chapter is the normal answer; the final ચોટ line (std-9 ch 11's "હું રામશંકરનો દીકરો છું.") is the
વળાંક and the પરિણામ together, and it may hold a topic of its own even though it is one sentence.
Padding a લઘુકથા up to a chapter-sized tree by splitting sentences is a cut error, not thoroughness.

## Emphasise

- **The turn.** Find the sentence where the story changes direction and build that topic around it.
  Name it in `topic_category: "climax"`.
- **Why a character chooses** — not only what they did. જોગીદાસ walks unarmed into the enemy's
  શોકસભા; આનંદી hands back the note she had prayed for. A વાર્તા teaches through motive, and the
  book's own written blocks ask for motive ("કેમ ?", "શા માટે ?").
- **બતાવવું, કહી દેવું નહીં.** Where the author shows a feeling through an action — ગોલુ's wet
  sacks laid on a sick calf (std-7 ch 10), the ડબ્બા-hunt comedy of two children running a kitchen
  (std-6 ch 11) — name that as craft, in one sentence, at the place it happens.
- **Dialogue as character.** How someone speaks is who they are: "બકા ! મને છોડાવ ને !" is a fish
  begging, and it is also the moment the tale turns magical.
- **The theme as something the story demonstrates.** std-6 ch 11 never says that housework is
  everyone's; it shows a brother learning to sweep. The explanation follows the demonstration.
- **In the લોકકથા and પૌરાણિક sub-forms**, add the teller's marks: the formulaic address (આપા), the
  repeated three-fold test, the flourish that survived because it was spoken aloud. These are craft,
  and they are the reason the story was remembered.

## Avoid (hard gate)

Agent 7 instantiates each of these as a per-topic testable sentence; Agent 13 blocks on any hard
item left unaddressed. Each check names the field it is checked against.

1. **Summary-only teaching.** `explanation` must add motive, craft or consequence to what
   `original_chunk` already narrates. **Check:** if `explanation` can be replaced by this topic's
   `modified_chunk` (the plain "what is happening" seed) with no loss of information, the topic
   fails. Severity: hard.
2. **A tacked-on બોધ.** No `explanation`, `summary`, `detailed_summary`, `concept_bullets` or
   recall `answer` may close on an abstract moral. **Check:** none of those fields contains a
   sentence of the form "આ વાર્તા આપણને શીખવે છે…" / "આપણે પણ … જોઈએ" / "બોધ એ છે કે…" that is not
   a verbatim quotation from the chapter. Where the chapter itself states the moral — std-7 P3's
   "લોભનો સ્પર્શ થયો એટલે એ અદૃશ્ય થઈ ગયાં !" — quote it and teach it as the author's move.
   Severity: hard.
3. **Judging a character the story treats with sympathy.** **Check:** no `explanation`,
   `real_life_example`, recall `answer` or exercise `answer` applies an evaluative label (ગાંડો,
   મૂરખ, ખોટો, ગુનેગાર, અંધશ્રદ્ધાળુ or a synonym) to a character the text presents with sympathy —
   આનંદીના પિતા માણેકલાલ (std-7 ch 12), the બહારવટિયો જોગીદાસ (std-7 ch 8), the family that makes a
   vow for a sick calf (std-7 ch 10). Severity: hard.
4. **Spoiling the turn early.** **Check:** no topic that precedes the topic carrying
   `topic_category: "climax"` names the outcome — the outcome's proper nouns and result words must
   not appear in an earlier topic's `explanation`, `summary` or recall `answer`. Severity: hard.
5. **Debunking or verifying a પૌરાણિક કથા.** The tale is taught as a tale. **Check:** no field
   asserts that the events did or did not happen, or corrects them against science ("ખરેખર એવું ન
   બને", "વૈજ્ઞાનિક રીતે…"). std-6 ch 13 is the model: the chapter itself holds the કથા and the
   science side by side, in that order, without either cancelling the other. Severity: hard.
6. **Standardising a લોકકથા's dialect.** **Check:** every તળપદો word that appears in
   `original_chunk` appears in the same form wherever the teaching quotes it — આપા, ખરખરો, ડાયરો,
   ગરાસ, ભે are glossed, never replaced by their માનક ગુજરાતી equivalents (std-7 ch 8, whose intro
   box orders exactly this: "આ પાઠમાં વપરાયેલા તળપદા શબ્દોનો બાળકોને ખાસ પરિચય કરાવવો."). Severity:
   hard.
7. **Inventing what the excerpt does not contain.** **Check:** for a નવલકથાખંડ or any excerpt, no
   field narrates an event absent from every `original_chunk` in the plan. Severity: hard.
8. **Crediting the translator as the author.** **Check:** where the page prints an અનુવાદ credit
   (std-7 ch 10 "અનુ. આશા વીરેન્દ્ર", std-10 ch 16 અનુવાદક પ્રસાદ બ્રહ્મભટ્ટ), the root
   `chapter_name`/attribution treats the original writer as the author and the translator as
   translator; both lines are transcribed as printed. Severity: soft (report at Agent 13).

## Priors

**Media** — one image per ઘટના, showing the **moment**, not the summary: the instant of the choice,
the faces at the turn. The depiction test is one sentence — if the picture cannot be described as a
single moment someone could photograph, it is a summary and must be re-authored. Characters stay
consistent across the chapter's images (same clothes, same age, same features); setting is Indian,
Gujarat-flavoured where the text is (a Saurashtra ડાયરો, a village courtyard, an ST-bus road). For
the પૌરાણિક sub-form, show the scene as the કથા tells it — બાળ હનુમાન reaching for a fruit-red sun —
never an iconographic worship portrait. For a frame story, the frame image and the embedded-tale
image must be visually distinguishable (classroom vs. sky), so the child can see which layer they
are in. `generation_prompt` is self-contained per `reference/field_shape_rules.md`; the narrator bar
carries one line of Gujarati in Gujarati script, named exactly. No Gujarati frame pool exists, so
every scene is authored: `image_url: ""`, `reuse_report.reused: 0`. At most one `2d_tool` per
chapter, and a story rarely needs it.

**સ્વાધ્યાય** — *measured, std 6–8*: વાતચીત (oral, usually carrying the counterfactual), an
event-ordering block, "પ્રશ્નોના જવાબ લખો" comprehension and motive questions, speaker attribution,
paraphrase→find-the-printed-line, sentence-making from chapter words, a રૂઢિપ્રયોગ block fed by the
box beside શબ્દાર્થ (std-7 ch 12's "નીચેના રેખાંકિત શબ્દજૂથને સ્થાને પાઠમાંના રૂઢિપ્રયોગ મૂકીને ફકરો
ફરીથી લખો :"), word-formation batteries, a પ્રવૃત્તિ/જૂથકાર્ય, and the standing L2 block
"નીચેનાં વાક્યોનો તમારી પ્રથમ ભાષામાં અનુવાદ કરો." A chapter-final green grammar box (સંજ્ઞા,
વાક્યના પ્રકારો, હકારવાચક-નકારવાચક…) feeds specific numbered blocks and is **teaching matter, not an
exercise**. *Measured at std 10 on ch 16 only*, and **provisional for the other std-10 વાર્તા
chapters**: the fixed ladder ✓-MCQ → એક-એક વાક્યમાં → બે-ત્રણ વાક્યમાં → સવિસ્તાર, followed by
વિદ્યાર્થી-પ્રવૃત્તિ, ભાષા-અભિવ્યક્તિ and શિક્ષકની ભૂમિકા — the last of which is addressed to the
teacher and is not a child-facing exercise. How topics prepare them: the topic holding the વળાંક
prepares the motive and counterfactual questions; cutting ઘટના-wise is itself what prepares the
ordering block; the topic that carries a character's speech prepares the attribution block. Name the
skill each topic prepares (`reference/teaching_block_format.md`). Agent 10 answers every printed
block; **no સ્વાધ્યાય block is ever a topic**, including a પૂરકવાચન unit that prints none at all
(std-7 P3 has an intro box and no સ્વાધ્યાય — that is normal, not a gap).

**Language** — everyday spoken શિષ્ટ ગુજરાતી carrying રૂઢિપ્રયોગ and કહેવત. From std 7 the story
chapters print a **રૂઢિપ્રયોગ box beside શબ્દાર્થ** (પલ્લું ભારે થવું, વહારે ધાવું, જીવ હથેળીમાં
લેવો, પગે પાંખો આવવી) — those idioms are exactly where an L2 child stalls, so they are glossed at
the point of first use and are first candidates for `key_terms` in `"શબ્દ — અર્થ"` form
(`reference/shabd_gloss.md`; the L2 bar for "everyday word" sits lower than a first-language pack's).
Register moves by sub-form and is never corrected, only explained (`reference/gujarati_verbatim.md`):
Saurashtra oral diction in a લોકકથા (સાદ વરત્યો, ખરખરો, ડાયરો), tatsama war and ritual vocabulary in
a પૌરાણિક કથા (શિરસ્ત્રાણ, બાણશય્યા, વીરગતિ — the book glosses them itself), English loanwords in
Gujarati script in a વિજ્ઞાન-કલ્પનકથા (ઍરટૅક્સી, સેન્સર, સ્માર્ટબોર્ડ), and household colloquialism
in a ઘરેલુ વાર્તા. Where a non-Gujarati verse is quoted inside the chapter's own apparatus (the
હનુમાન ચાલીસા couplet in std-6 ch 13's સ્વાધ્યાય, Avadhi), transcribe as printed and gloss it — do
not treat it as Gujarati and do not translate it into the plan's prose.

**Sensitivity** — this form needs the paragraph. GSEB's વાર્તા chapters put real difficulty on the
page: a hero's death and revenge framing (std-7 ch 3), poverty and a parent's mental illness in
period phrasing "ગાંડાની ઇસ્પિતાલ" (std-7 ch 12), an outlaw treated with honour (std-7 ch 8), a folk
vow and a "ચમત્કાર" the doctor's medicine quietly explains (std-7 ch 10), myth taught inside a
science-friendly frame (std-6 ch 13), and God-questions as the engine of a wit tale (std-7 ch 2).
Agent 8 **flags and guides, never censors**: it says how to teach the passage, not whether. Keep the
book's own dignity register, use communities' own names, and do not turn a story into a debate about
belief. Area labels in Gujarati per the pack convention (ધર્મ, સમુદાય, ક્ષેત્ર, વિકલાંગતા, સંઘર્ષ,
જાતિ-ભૂમિકા, સુરક્ષા).

## Recall priors

Two to three per topic, Bloom-laddered, each with a real model answer, `bloom_level` lowercase:

- one **remember** — શું બન્યું: an event the topic's own `original_chunk` states.
- one **understand** — પાત્રે એમ કેમ કર્યું: motive, answerable from this topic, not from the whole
  chapter.
- one **analyze** or **apply** on the topic that carries the વળાંક — વળાંક શું બતાવે છે, or the
  book's own counterfactual turned into an answerable question. The model answer stays
  text-grounded: where the સ્વાધ્યાય invites speculation ("…હોત તો શું થાત ?"), the answer reasons
  from what the text established, and says so.

Recall answers are bound by the same hard gates as `explanation`: no appended બોધ, no judging a
sympathetic character, no event the excerpt does not contain.
