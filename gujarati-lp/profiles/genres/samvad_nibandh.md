## Diagnosis signals

Prose that **argues**. Either two voices in sustained conversation carried inside the prose
("double quotes" in running paragraphs, speech attributed in the sentence itself), or one voice
making a case. There is no plot worth the name — nothing turns, nobody chooses; the movement is
in the argument. Themes cluster on પર્યાવરણ, સમાજ, નાગરિક જવાબદારી, ભાષા-સંસ્કૃતિ.

The strongest tell is signal C, the સ્વાધ્યાય: it asks the child **what each side says**, whether
they agree and why, and then to argue or write their own view. Measured instances:

- std-6 ch-6, block 6 — "આપેલાં વાક્યો વાંચો, વિચારો અને તે કોણ બોલતું હશે, તે લખો." (7 attribution items)
- std-6 ch-6, block 15 — "બે-બે મિત્રોની જોડી બનાવો. એક પુરુષોત્તમભાઈ અને બીજા સવજીભાઈ બનીને પાઠના
  સંવાદ બોલો. પછી પાત્ર બદલીને ફરી સંવાદ કરો." (both roles, then swapped)
- std-8 ch-7, ચર્ચા-વિચારણા box — "'લગ્ન સાદાઈથી કરવાં જોઈએ કે નહીં' • બે પક્ષ બનાવી ચર્ચા કરાવવી.
  ચર્ચામાંથી નીકળી આવતા મુદ્દાઓને આધારે નિબંધ લખવા કહેવું." (two sides → debate → essay, in one box)
- std-9 ch-6, વિદ્યાર્થી-પ્રવૃત્તિ — a ચર્ચાસભા on 'ગુજરાતી ભાષાની સજ્જતા વધે'; its શિક્ષકની ભૂમિકા
  note itself lays out both positions (અંગ્રેજી માધ્યમનો આગ્રહ against ભાષા-સંરક્ષણની જવાબદારી).

Where the ચર્ચા-વિચારણા box (std-8/9/10 readers) carries a two-sided proposition, that box alone
is enough to raise this profile as a candidate — but it never overrides signal A.

## Lens

**તર્ક + દૃષ્ટિકોણ + જવાબદારી**

Two positions, and what the text asks of the reader who has heard both.

## Guiding question

*બંને પક્ષ શું કહે છે, અને એ મારી પાસે શું માગે છે ?*

A shape, not a string to copy. Each chapter derives its own from its own proposition — std-6 ch-6
would ask about a village that changed and who changed it; std-9 ch-6 about what is lost when a
language goes. Agent 1 writes it from THIS chapter or it is wrong.

## Explanation unit — **one move of the argument**

A move is: a position stated, a challenge to it, a concession, or the ask. One move = one topic.

- **સંવાદ:** an exchange that completes one move — a question and the answer that settles it.
  Never cut mid-move; never bundle three unrelated points into one topic. Where one speaker holds
  the floor for several paragraphs to make a single point, that whole run is ONE topic and the
  internal steps go into `concepts[]`, not into extra topics.
- **નિબંધ:** one strand of the case. std-9 ch-6 is built as three London visit-anecdotes followed
  by the તર્ક they support — each anecdote is a strand, the તર્ક is the strand that lands them.

Both speakers' words stay verbatim in `original_chunk`, with the printed quotation marks and the
printed attribution clause intact. Strip only the `[[…]]` scaffolding markers.

**Refrain analogue.** Prose argument has no ટેક, but these chapters often carry one sentence the
text returns to (std-10 ch-6: "સામગ્રી તો સમાજની છે ને !"). Quote it inside the topic where it
lands and reference it where it returns — it never becomes a topic of its own, and it is never
paraphrased into a slogan.

## How the argument moves, and therefore how it is cut

Read the passage for its four moves before drawing a single boundary:

1. **સ્થાપના** — someone puts a position on the table. (std-6 ch-6: પુરુષોત્તમભાઈ returns after
   અઢી દાયકા and does not recognise the village; the question "આ કેમ થયું ?" opens the case.)
2. **પડકાર / પ્રશ્ન** — the other voice pushes back, or asks for evidence.
3. **સ્વીકાર / પુરાવો** — a concession, or the evidence: what was actually done —
   નક્ષત્રવન, ઔષધબાગ, કિચનગાર્ડન.
4. **માગણી** — the ask the text leaves with the reader: પર્યાવરણનું જતન એ ફરજ છે.

A topic boundary that falls anywhere other than between two moves is a bad cut, and Agent 4 will
see it as a topic whose `explanation` has to describe two different things.

## Emphasise

- **Both sides, fairly.** Even where the chapter plainly favours one, the other position is stated
  as its holder would state it. A flattened opponent teaches a child that arguing is about winning.
- **The move each turn makes** — objecting, conceding, giving evidence, asking. Naming the move is
  what turns a reading into a transferable skill; the child needs the name before the debate task.
- **What the text asks of the reader.** These chapters end in a જવાબદારી. State it as the text
  states it, attributed to the text ("પાઠ કહે છે…"), not issued by the teaching voice.
- **સજીવારોપણ where a non-human speaks.** A river or a tree that argues is a device, and the child
  should see the choice: giving it a voice makes its case impossible to ignore.
- **Everyday reasoning.** The best hand-over shows the same argument inside the child's own week —
  શેરીમાં કચરો, વર્ગખંડનો પંખો, ઘરનું પાણી.
- **The words the argument runs on.** These chapters carry abstract vocabulary an L2 child stops on
  (સંવર્ધન, દરકાર, સંકલ્પ, તટસ્થ, ઓતપ્રોત). Gloss at point of first use, inline, in the explanation.

## Avoid (hard gate)

Agent 7 instantiates each of these as a per-topic testable sentence; Agent 13 blocks on `severity:
"hard"`. Structural item G6 is checked at Agent 4.

- **G1 — Both voices present.** For any topic whose `original_chunk` contains quoted words from two
  speakers, `explanation` must name both speakers and report what each of them says. Check: both
  speaker strings from `original_chunk` occur in `explanation`. One-voice summary of a two-voice
  passage is a hard fail.
- **G2 — No preaching.** No `explanation`, `summary`, `brief_summary`, `detailed_summary`,
  `real_life_example`, `publication_text` or recall `answer` may contain a second-person directive
  to the child (`…કરો`, `…ન કરો`, `તમારે … જોઈએ`, `આપણે … જોઈએ`). The text's ask is reported, never
  issued. Check: no field matches the directive patterns Agent 7 lists from THIS chapter.
- **G3 — No flattened opponent.** No field may attach a character-judging word (મૂર્ખ, બેજવાબદાર,
  અજ્ઞાની, ખરાબ, સ્વાર્થી, and the per-chapter list Agent 7 derives) to a named speaker or to the
  side the chapter disagrees with. Their reason is stated; only the reason may be weighed.
- **G4 — Dialogue stays dialogue.** No `original_chunk` may convert quoted speech into reported
  speech. Check: the count of opening quotation marks in `original_chunk` equals the count on the
  rendered page for that span, and every attribution clause printed with the quote is present.
- **G5 — No guilt.** No field may assign the harm to the child, their family, or their community.
  `real_life_example` must land on one action a std-N child can actually take this week; a field
  that ends on a consequence the child cannot prevent is a hard fail. Check: Agent 7 names the
  action for each such topic; Agent 13 verifies a doable action is named.
- **G6 — Wrong-route gate (checked at Agent 4).** If `00_chapter_normalized.md` contains any line
  matching a printed speaker label in label position (`^\s*\S+\s*:` used as a speaker tag) or any
  parenthetical stage direction, this profile is the wrong route: block and reroute to
  `natak_ekanki.md`. Check: that count is zero wherever this profile is the dominant genre.
- **G7 — Argument, not payload.** No topic may be cut on a fact boundary. If the topics of a
  chapter routed here are separable by *what they inform about* rather than by *what move they
  make*, the diagnosis is wrong: reroute to `mahitiprad_gadya.md`. Check: every topic's
  `topic_name` names a move or a position, never a fact-bundle label.

## Priors

**Media** — one image per reading scene, showing **both parties together in the same frame** so the
conversation is visible; put the disputed thing between them where the chapter gives you one (the
bare land and the planted land in std-6 ch-6 — the book itself uses photographs of the નક્ષત્રવન
and the ઔષધબાગ). For a single-voice નિબંધ, image the concrete anecdote the strand rests on, never
the abstraction: std-9 ch-6 draws the London sitting-room where a Gujarati family speaks English
to a Spanish priest, not "language and culture". Where a non-human speaks, draw it with presence
but without a cartoon face unless the chapter's own tone supports it. `generation_prompt` is
self-contained; narrator bar carries one exact Gujarati-script line. Standard limits hold: one
image per reading scene, at most one `2d_tool` per chapter, 16:9, `image_url: ""` until generated.

**સ્વાધ્યાય** — expect, from the measured blocks above: a dialogue-attribution block ("તે કોણ
બોલતું હશે"), plain comprehension `નીચેના પ્રશ્નોના ઉત્તર લખો`, an agree-and-justify question
("…કેવી રીતે ?"), a pair or group role-play of the same conversation with roles swapped, a
જૂથ ચર્ચા whose points are reported to the class, and — in std-8/9/10 — a ચર્ચા-વિચારણા box that
may run debate into essay. Vocabulary and grammar blocks sit alongside them and still map to
topics. Exercises are NEVER topics; Agent 10 owns every block. Where a topic prepares the debate,
its `explanation` must name the move the child will need there — giving a reason, answering an
objection, conceding a point — so the skill is taught before it is tested. Agent 10 maps each
argumentative item to the topics that prepare it via `covered_by_topics[]`, and reports unmapped
items rather than inventing a mapping.

**Language** — accessible modern શિષ્ટ ગુજરાતી carrying domain vocabulary (પર્યાવરણ, સંવર્ધન,
હરિયાળી, દરકાર, સંકલ્પ, નાગરિક, જવાબદારી) and, at std 9–10, abstract Sanskritic words that stop an
L2 reader cold (તટસ્થ, ઓતપ્રોત, સ્થિતપ્રજ્ઞ, સાત્ત્વિકતા). Gloss at point of use per
`reference/shabd_gloss.md`; the L2 stopping-point bar sits lower than a first-language pack's, so
gloss more, not less. `key_terms` in `"શબ્દ — અર્થ"` form, 3–6 per topic. The printed શબ્દાર્થ box
is evidence of what the book thinks is hard, not a source to copy wholesale. Verbatim discipline
per `reference/gujarati_verbatim.md`: Gujarati script only, `.` as the full stop — the
Devanagari daṇḍa is never introduced into a Gujarati chapter — quotation marks and attribution
clauses exactly as printed, no transliteration.

**Sensitivity** — this form argues about how people live, so it reaches sensitive ground more often
than any other prose genre here: std-9 ch-6 runs on અંગ્રેજી માધ્યમ vs માતૃભાષા, a live family
decision for many students; std-8's કંકોતરી debate touches wedding spending across communities of
very different means; std-10 ch-6 judges behaviour in a European setting. Agent 8 flags and guides,
never censors, using the standard area labels (ધર્મ, સમુદાય, ક્ષેત્ર, જાતિ-ભૂમિકા). Guidance
principle for this form: a child whose family sits on the side the chapter argues against must be
able to read the topic without being described as wrong. Teach the position, weigh the reason.

## Recall priors

Two to three `recall_questions` per topic, each with a real model answer, in the exact shape
`{"id":"{topic_id}.RQ{n}","legacy_id":"{topic_id}.TR{n}","prompt","answer","difficulty","bloom_level"}`
(`bloom_level` lowercase here). For this form the ladder is:

1. **`remember`** — what does each side claim? (Answerable from the topic's `original_chunk` alone;
   where the topic holds two voices, the model answer states both claims.)
2. **`understand`** — what reason or evidence is given for it, and what does that reason rest on?
3. **`evaluate`** — which side convinces you, and why? The model answer must be a real answer that
   commits to a position and gives its reason; it must also make clear that the other answer is
   available. Never a model answer that merely restates the chapter's preference as the only
   defensible view.

At the segment level, one `{segment_id}.RQ{n}` question that asks the child to lay the two positions
side by side before choosing — the child must be able to state BOTH positions before being asked to
pick one. That is the ordering constraint this whole profile exists to protect.
