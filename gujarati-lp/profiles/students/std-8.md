# Student profile — Std 8 (GSEB ગુજરાતી, દ્વિતીય ભાષા)

**What this file is.** The per-standard learner profile for std 8: who the learner is, which teaching methods the plan must follow, the three understanding-level tiers (સહાય / ધોરણ / પ્રગત) as operational authoring dials, and the three ready-to-paste tutor system prompts.

**Sources.** Pedagogy: `PEDAGOGY.md` §1 (Std 8), §2 (tier model), §3 (Std 8 × tier prompts), §4 (pipeline implications). Corpus: `reference/corpus/std-8_inventory.md` — 21 units read off rendered pages (the GSEB PDFs are image-only; every corpus fact below is transcription, not extraction). Where the pedagogy report and the measured inventory disagree about what this book contains, **the inventory wins** and the disagreement is logged in §6.

**What this file never does.** It changes authored prose only. It does not add, rename, or drop a single JSON key, id, or band. The phase-2 contract (`reference/phase2_contract.md`) and the field bands (`reference/field_shape_rules.md`) are identical for all three tiers.

## 1. Who this learner is

**Age / stage.** ≈ 13 years old. Early formal operational: holds two variables at once, follows if-then chains, and reaches its first real comfort with counterfactuals. Metacognitive skill begins its sharp 13–14 growth, so strategy talk becomes teachable rather than decorative. Attention block ≈ 12–15 minutes (chat equivalent: three-sentence explanation, then something to do).

**Curricular position — the last year before the switch.** The NEP/NCF stage boundary at std 8 → std 9 is the pipeline's hard switch. Std 8 is still Middle Stage: fluency and consolidation, activity-first exercises, grammar met inside the chapter. Everything named — અલંકાર, છંદ, સમાસ, સાહિત્યપ્રકાર tags, the exam answer-length ladder — belongs to std 9 and later. Std 8 is where the *habits* those skills need (interpretation, explicit rule statement, self-editing) are built without the labels.

**L2 proficiency expectation.** CEFR ≈ **A2 / A2+ by the end of std 8** on a second language begun early — provisional, from the research track, not measured against GSEB learners. Assume a three-level spread inside every classroom (§3). Heritage-oral learners run listening and speaking roughly one level above reading and writing for the first years — track the skills separately and never infer reading strength from fluent speech.

**What the measured book demands of that learner.** From `reference/corpus/std-8_inventory.md`:

- **21 teaching units**: ch 1–7, R1, ch 8–15, R2, then પૂરક વાચન P1–P4. R1 and R2 are all-exercise revision blocks with no reading scene — they route to exercise handling (Agent 10), never to topic cutting.
- **Genre range is wide and mostly prose**: પ્રાર્થનાગીત, પૌરાણિક કથા, હાસ્યનિબંધ, વિસ્મયકથા, લલિતનિબંધ, ચરિત્રલેખ, લગ્નગીત (લોકગીત), સાહસકથા, વાર્તારૂપ સંવાદ, ગઝલ, આત્મકથા-ખંડ, એકાંકી (નાટક), સંવેદનકથા, ઘટના-સંવાદ, હાસ્યપ્રસંગ. Every genre prior is a prior — Agent 1 re-diagnoses from the rendered page.
- **Two new text grains appear this year**: a full એકાંકી with cast list, stage directions and `નામ : વાક્ય` dialogue (ch 12 ક્ષિતિ, 14 pdf pages — the longest unit), and a ગઝલ built of શેર couplets on the radif "દરિયો" with the poet's છાપ in the maqta (ch 10 હલેસે હલેસે). Neither is labelled with craft terminology in the book, and the tutor must not label them either.
- **Medieval verse arrives quoted inside modern prose** — નરસિંહ મહેતા's છાપ line in ch 6, another નરસિંહ line inside the essay in ch 3. Two grains in one chapter: never correct the older grain, never blend it into the modern one.
- **Density is up sharply**: 7-page prose chapters, dense run-on શબ્દાર્થ boxes, રૂઢિપ્રયોગ pre-blocks in 11 of 15 chapters (2–10 idioms each), Marathi sentences kept verbatim in ch 11, English medical vocabulary taught deliberately in ch 4, Roman abbreviations in the body of ch 14, and rustic spoken forms with apostrophe elision in chs 13 and 15.
- **The exercise apparatus is activity-first and prints no સ્વાધ્યાય banner at all.** Exercises begin unlabeled as numbered pink headings straight after the yellow શબ્દાર્થ box. Item 1 is **વાતચીત.** in 15 of 15 chapters; the last numbered item is **L1 translation** ("આપેલા ફકરાનો તમારી પ્રથમ ભાષામાં અનુવાદ કરો.") in 15 of 15 — a standing, printed sanction for translanguaging at this standard. A **ચર્ચા-વિચારણા** box closes every chapter.
- **Grammar is present everywhere and named nowhere**: કાળ transformation, ability/impersonal/દ્વારા passives, સંયોજક joining, અનુસ્વાર minimal pairs (ભાંગી/ભાગી, જંગ-જગ, વંદન-વદન), જોડાક્ષર hunts, agentive suffix grids, sentence-type wheels. Not one અલંકાર, છંદ or સમાસ label appears in the measured book — the pedagogy report's genre gate and the printed book agree.

**What changed vs std 7.**

| Dimension | Std 7 | Std 8 |
|---|---|---|
| Abstraction | motive inference; hypotheticals inside the story's concrete world | + interpretation, alternative endings, counterfactuals ("જો X ન બન્યું હોત તો?"); single-line verbal irony inside a story is now fair |
| Text | longer narratives, travelogue, first drama excerpt | denser social/biographical prose, memoir, a full એકાંકી, a ગઝલ; two-clause complex sentences, reported speech, quoted medieval verse |
| Craft vocabulary | ~zero labels; genres experienced | still no અલંકાર/છંદ/સમાસ labels; structural *pattern play* begins (refrain shapes, પ્રાસ hunts, couplet structure) |
| Grammar | inductive discovery, rule named lightly after the pattern | explicit rule **after** the encounter, plus a contrastive L1 note (ગુજરાતી SOV vs English SVO, અનુગ vs prepositions) |
| Metacognition | implicit | explicit: predict–monitor–fix, self-edit rubrics, first scaffolded "revise your own draft" |
| Writing | અખબારી નોંધ enters | પત્ર, simple નિબંધ, વાર્તાલેખન from points, અહેવાલ (ch 13 asks for 100–150 words) with self-edit checklists |

## 2. Method mix for this standard

The std-8 slogan from the report: **"Name it after you've met it."**

1. **Explicit rule statement AFTER contextual encounter.** Sequence, always: chapter encounter → learner attempt → explicit rule in one line → contrastive L1 note → practice on this chapter's own sentences. Never rule-first, never an invented example sentence — the measured book itself drills on its own text (ch 3's present→past paragraph, ch 6's passive transforms, ch 15's તેથી-joining).
2. **Translanguaging, used deliberately.** Highest-value at this stage (secondary-school effect g = 1.165). Discuss in English, produce in Gujarati. The book prints an L1-translation item at the end of every single chapter — treat that as the sanctioned shape, not a workaround.
3. **Metacognition taught aloud.** Predict before reading, mark confusion while reading, fix with the શબ્દાર્થ box after. Self-editing rubrics are always *provided*; nothing self-assessed is left unscaffolded.
4. **Interpretation becomes fair game.** Character motive, alternative endings, the moral in the learner's own words. Not unstated-theme extraction with evidence citing — that is a std-9 skill.
5. **Identity and personal response.** Engagement peaks 13–15: opinion-on-character, diary-of-a-character, "તમારા મત પ્રમાણે તે યોગ્ય છે ? શા માટે ?" (ch 11 prints exactly this).
6. **Writing turns real,** with a declared target feature per task and feedback on that feature only.

**Top-5 DO / DON'T (report §1, Std 8).**

| # | DO | DON'T |
|---|----|----|
| 1 | Sequence every grammar item: chapter encounter → learner attempt → explicit rule → contrastive L1 note → practice on chapter sentences. | Don't teach named-device identification (અલંકાર/છંદ/સમાસ labels) — that starts Std 9. |
| 2 | Teach the predict–monitor–fix routine and self-editing rubrics; first "revise your own draft" tasks live here, scaffolded. | Don't assign unscaffolded self-assessment; rubric always provided. |
| 3 | Ask for the lesson/moral **in the student's own words**; offer alternative-ending and "what if" tasks on the story's own world. | Don't ask for unstated-theme extraction with evidence citing (Std 9 skill). |
| 4 | Exploit identity/personal-response engagement (peaks 13–15): opinion-on-character, diary-of-character tasks. | Don't run solo public performance as assessment; prefer pair/group formats, low-stakes oral checks. |
| 5 | Use syllable-counting and couplet structure as playful pattern work. | Don't front-load full satirical texts; single-line irony only. |

Std-8-specific reading of DO 5 against the measured book: the report's હાઈકુ/દુહા examples are not in this reader (§6). The pattern-play material that *is* printed here is the refrain shape of ch 1 and ch 7, the પ્રાસ hunts (ch 1 §4, ch 10 §8), and the શેર/radif repetition of ch 10. Use those.

One further std-8 hazard the corpus forces: **ch 3 is literary humour with irony**, which is L2-hard, and ch 15 narrates in spoken dialect throughout. Both stay in scope — the tier changes the on-ramp, not the text.

### Cultural anchoring for this standard

Every `real_life_example` (and any example the tutor improvises) is anchored in Gujarat's own
world — the bank is `reference/gujarat_cultural_anchors.md`, already passed with every generation
call (§5). Std 8 is the age-ladder's middle rung: the child's own street still anchors best, but
the **ગામ/શહેર and its work** now open up — livelihoods heard and seen daily, festivals as lived
neighbourly moments, the state's crafts met as sound and touch rather than as heritage labels.
The std-8 step is **somebody else's hands**: a std-7 anchor is something that happened to the
child, a std-8 anchor is a piece of work the child stood and watched long enough to see the steps
in it. That is what makes it carry patience, skill and accumulation — and it matches the year's
own move, "name it after you've met it" (§2).

**Choosing one.** Name the scene's ભાવ, then take the row.

| Scene's ભાવ | The std-8 anchor that carries it |
|---|---|
| ધીરજથી, તાર-તારથી બંધાતું આખું | સાળ પર સામસામા જતા તાણા-વાણા ને દિવસભરનો ખટ-ખટ |
| ટીપે ટીપે સરોવર | દૂધમંડળીની સવારની લાઇન, ડેરીમાં ખખડતાં કૅન |
| સૌના ભેગા બળે ભારે વસ્તુ ચાલે | રથયાત્રાનું દોરડું ખેંચતા હજારો હાથ |
| ભૂલ કબૂલવી, જાત સામે જોવું | પર્યુષણના ઘરમાં શાંત આઠ દિવસ ને `મિચ્છા મિ દુક્કડં` |
| પડોશની જુદી જુદી રીતો | નવરોઝને દિવસે પડોશીના ઉંબરે થતો ચોકનો શણગાર |
| હિંમત, ને કિનારે રાહ | વહેલી સવારે નીકળતી માછીની હોડી, ને કિનારે ઊભું રહેતું ઘર |

The standing rules do not move: exactly ONE example per scene, 55–90 words, inside the child's own
experience; the anchor illustrates the text's FEELING and never adds a claim about the text, the
poet, or the chapter. Rotate domains across a chapter, and across the book show the
whole state — Saurashtra, Kutch, North/Central/South Gujarat, coast and tribal belt, city and
ફળિયું — and all its communities, never one slice, never a costume.

**The std-8 failure mode: the heritage-label anchor.** The craft or the community becomes a display
case — admired from outside, with no hands in it. One sentence out of the middle of an example:

- ✅ `સાળ પર તાણો-વાણો સામસામા જાય ને ધીરે ધીરે કાપડ બંધાય; ખટ-ખટ અવાજ આખો દિવસ ચાલે.`
- ❌ `ગુજરાત હસ્તકલાનો સમૃદ્ધ વારસો ધરાવે છે; પટોળાં ને બાંધણી તેની શાન છે.`

The ❌ half fails for a nameable reason: nothing in it is seen, heard or touched, so there is no
feeling for the text to lean on — it praises a heritage instead of showing a pair of hands. It is
also one step from costume tokenism, which the bank's anti-patterns rule out outright.

Tier gradient: **સહાય** uses only the bank's most familiar, most concrete anchors;
**ધોરણ** the everyday spread including
street-level work; **પ્રગત** may carry a less common anchor (અજરખના છાપ-બીબાની થપ-થપ, કચ્છી ભરતના
ટાંકે ટાંકે ચમકતાં આભલાં) provided it needs no glossary.

## 3. The three tiers

Three tiers, fixed across all standards, named in Gujarati and shared by every generator and by the tutor runtime:

- **સહાય (Support)** — below-grade understanding. Expected population ~15–25%.
- **ધોરણ (Core)** — at-grade. Expected population ~60–80%.
- **પ્રગત (Advanced)** — above-grade. Expected population ~5–15%.

*(Population shares are the research track's, provisional — not measured on GSEB std-8 cohorts.)*

**Assignment rule — pre-test cut scores.** A short unit pre-test decides the tier:

| Pre-test score | Tier | Handling |
|---|---|---|
| ≥ 80–85% | પ્રગત | compacting — skip mastered blocks, go to extension |
| ~50–80% | ધોરણ | full plan as authored |
| < 50%, or missing prerequisites | સહાય | full plan with the support scaffolds of this section |

Re-sort **per unit, or every 4–8 weeks**. Tiers are flexible readiness states, not labels. Tier ≠ intelligence.

**The two invariants that make the model honest.**

- **Bloom is never capped for સહાય.** Every tier reaches analyze and evaluate; only the on-ramp differs. A સહાય learner reaches interpretation through a two-column chart, a given frame, or a decomposed chain of choices — never through a lowered ceiling. A tier that quietly becomes "less learning" instead of "more support" is a defect, and the routing owner is Agent 12.
- **FIXED across tiers:** the chapter, the learning outcome (અધ્યયન નિષ્પત્તિ), the big idea, the assessment target, and access to higher-order thinking. **Authoring order: write the પ્રગત variant first, then scaffold down.** Writing સહાય first produces dilution; scaffolding down produces access.

**Operational descriptors — std 8.** (Tomlinson's Equalizer dials, instantiated for this standard's texts.)

| Dial | સહાય | ધોરણ | પ્રગત |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Vocabulary load** | 2–3 new words per lesson on the full 8-step routine (definition, 2 examples, 2 non-examples, use, spaced review) + picture/L1 support; **also gloss the everyday and polysemous words the text assumes** — at std 8 that means the તળપદા of ch 7/13/15, the kinship terms of ch 12, and the Marathi lines of ch 11 | 2–3 new words per lesson in depth (8–10/week), academic (Tier-2) words prioritised; તળપદા glossed | same core words + morphology and word-family extension (-નાર, -વાળું, -પણું/-તા, અ-/બિન-/ગેર-, the agentive suffixes ch 12 §6 prints); rare and તત્સમ words as stretch |
| **Sentence cap in explanations** | ≤ 8–10 words per sentence, simple and compound only, one idea per sentence; say–show–do redundancy | grade cap: two-clause sentences and reported speech acceptable; say once, check once | may run one notch above the grade cap; slightly denser register, still clear |
| **Gloss density** | every low-frequency word gets an inline appositive gloss **plus** an English equivalent ("વ્યક્તિત્વ — એટલે કે સ્વભાવ, personality"); running bilingual glossary re-quizzed each session | new / તળપદા / academic words glossed inline in Gujarati at point of first use; English gloss on request | minimal — student infers from context first, tutor confirms, then extends to the word family or one chapter રૂઢિપ્રયોગ |
| **Abstraction** | concrete end of every dial; interpretation arrives **pre-structured** (a two-column chart "પાત્રે શું કર્યું / કેમ કર્યું હશે"), worked templates, familiar contexts | concrete anchor first, then the grade-level interpretation extracted with guidance | may start from the principle and ask for instances; far transfer ("આ પાત્ર જેવું વર્તન આજે ક્યાં જોવા મળે?"), cross-scene links |
| **Examples** | 3–4 worked examples from familiar Gujarat life **before** any practice item; examples drawn from the curated Gujarat bank only | 1–2 worked examples → example-problem pair → independent problem | one model, then self-derivation; non-examples and edge cases; unfamiliar contexts allowed |
| **Question ladder** | choice and yes/no → wh- about actions and stated feelings → structured interpretation: motive offered as a choice, moral as a frame ("આ વાર્તા શીખવે છે કે ___"), alternative endings offered as options to pick and justify in one sentence. Analysis is reached **through the chart, never through an open prompt** | literal → motive with cited hints → interpretation ("પાત્રે આમ કેમ કર્યું? બીજો અંત કેવો હોઈ શકે?") → moral **in the student's own words** → light counterfactual ("જો X ન બન્યું હોત તો?"); single-line irony may be noticed | starts at interpretation: motive with evidence, alternative endings **written** not chosen, moral plus a counter-case, counterfactual chains; craft noticed without labels ("કવિ કઈ પંક્તિમાં નિર્જીવ વસ્તુને જીવતી બતાવે છે?") |
| **Pacing** | 2 short sentences per turn, then an action; one micro-step per turn; comprehension check every turn; **2–3 new interacting elements max**; extended I-do / We-do | 10/2 rhythm — up to 3 sentences, then the learner does something; **≤ 4 new elements**; I-do → We-do → You-do | probe first and skip the known; segments of 4–5 sentences; may enter at You-do; long independent tasks with a rubric |
| **Feedback style** | flag → name the category in plain terms (જોડણી / કાળ / લિંગ-વચન / શબ્દક્રમ) → give the correct form → one retry on the same point. **One feature per task**, praise the attempt first | flag → name the category → correct → retry. Personal-response tasks get content praise first and **one** language point second | self-correction prompted first; category named only if needed; recast into a higher register and invite imitation; error log kept across sessions |
| **Media / support** | picture support and audio-assisted repeated reading by default; sentence frames with one slot | visuals for key concepts; frames with 2–3 slots that retire as proficiency rises | text-first; media only where the form demands it (hearing the ગઝલ's rhythm); no frames |
| **Translanguaging** | generous — concept explanation in English allowed, production always in Gujarati; explicit L1 contrast notes | English for grammar explanation and quick equivalents; discussion drifts back to Gujarati | Gujarati throughout; English only on request or for a sharp grammar contrast |
| **Metacognition** | checklist-shaped only: "ન સમજાયેલા શબ્દો નીચે લીટી કરો", a given 3-item self-edit list | predict–monitor–fix taught aloud; a self-edit rubric applied by the student **before** the tutor corrects | full strategy talk — the student explains their method, keeps an error log, self-edits before the draft is seen |

**Rules that hold at every tier (std 8).** No અલંકાર / છંદ / સમાસ labels. No unstated-theme extraction with evidence citing. No solo public performance as assessment. Nothing supplied from model memory — no poet facts, no meanings, no "traditional" readings; grammar examples come from this chapter's own sentences. One new interacting element per block (never new vocabulary and new grammar together). Two-attempt rule before direct explanation.

## 4. The three tutor system prompts

Paste one block as the tutor session's system prompt. The chapter text, the શબ્દાર્થ box, and the chapter's exercise items must already be in context verbatim — these prompts assume it. Re-inject the block, or at minimum its REGISTER / QUESTIONING / PACING fields, with **every** generation call: prompt-level difficulty control decays within about nine turns.

### Std 8 × સહાય

```
ROLE: You are a steady, encouraging Gujarati (second language) tutor for a GSEB Std 8 student below grade level (સહાય tier). Std 8 texts get denser (biographical prose, bhakti verse), so your job is access, not dilution: same ideas, smaller steps.

SOURCE FIDELITY: Teach only from the chapter text, glossary, and સ્વાધ્યાય provided here; quote lines verbatim. Never recall texts/poets/meanings from memory; never invent examples — use chapter sentences.

SCRIPT: Gujarati in Gujarati script only (U+0A80–U+0AFF); no Devanagari, no romanized Gujarati. "જવાબ ગુજરાતી લિપિમાં લખો."

REGISTER: Simple Gujarati; English allowed for explaining ideas and grammar contrast, production always in Gujarati. Sentences ≤10 words.

EXPLANATION: 2 short sentences per turn, then action. Pre-teach the 2–3 needed words BEFORE reading a segment. For verse (પદ/સૉનેટ exposure), read line-by-line: you read, gloss, the student restates in their own simple words.

GLOSSING: Every hard/તળપદા word inline: Gujarati appositive + English. Maintain and re-quiz a running glossary each session.

EXAMPLES: 3–4 worked examples from familiar life before practice; interpretation tasks arrive pre-structured (a two-column chart: "પાત્રે શું કર્યું / કેમ કર્યું હશે").

CULTURAL ANCHORING: Anchor every example in Gujarat's own world per reference/gujarat_cultural_anchors.md, using only its most familiar, most concrete anchors (દૂધમંડળીની સવારની લાઇન, ખેતરના કૂવે ડોલનો ઘડૂલિયો અવાજ, આંગણે રાતની ખાટલા-વાત, ચોમાસાનું પહેલું ઝાપટું). The anchor shows the text's feeling only; it never adds facts about the text, the poet, or the chapter.

QUESTIONING: Choice and yes/no first, then wh-, then structured interpretation: motive as a choice, moral as a frame ("આ વાર્તા શીખવે છે કે ___"). Alternative endings offered as options to pick and justify with one sentence. You still reach analysis — through the chart, never through open prompts.

GRAMMAR: Meet the form in a chapter sentence, attempt, THEN a one-line explicit rule + one English contrast ("ગુજરાતીમાં ક્રિયાપદ છેલ્લે આવે; English puts the verb in the middle"). One rule at a time.

METACOGNITION: Checklist-shaped only: "ન સમજાયેલા શબ્દો નીચે લીટી કરો," "લખ્યા પછી આ 3 વસ્તુ તપાસો" (a given 3-item self-edit list).

FEEDBACK: Flag → name category (જોડણી / કાળ / લિંગ-વચન / શબ્દક્રમ) → correct form → one retry. One feature per task.

PACING & ANSWERS: 2-attempt rule then direct explanation. Never more than 2–3 new elements per segment. Group-feel formats: "ચાલો સાથે વાંચીએ" beats "એકલા વાંચી બતાવો."
```

### Std 8 × ધોરણ

CULTURAL ANCHORING: Anchor examples in Gujarat's own world per reference/gujarat_cultural_anchors.md — everyday child life, festivals and food as lived moments, and the ગામ/શહેરનું કામ the child can watch one pair of hands doing (ડેરીમાં ખખડતાં કૅન, વહેલી સવારે નીકળતી માછીની હોડી, રથયાત્રાના દોરડે હજારો હાથ), drawn from across the state's regions and communities. ### Std 8 × પ્રગત

```
ROLE: You are a Gujarati (second language) tutor for a GSEB Std 8 student above grade level (પ્રગત tier).

SOURCE FIDELITY: Provided chapter material only; verbatim quotes; nothing from memory — no poets, no meanings, no "traditional" interpretations. Chapter sentences are the only grammar examples.

REGISTER: Rich natural Gujarati throughout; English only on request or for a sharp grammar contrast the student will enjoy.

EXPLANATION: Probe-first compacting: quick diagnostic question, skip the known, spend time on the interesting. Segments up to 4–5 sentences; the student should talk more than you.

GLOSSING: Infer-from-context first; confirm; extend into word families and one chapter રૂઢિપ્રયોગ; invite the student to coin a sentence with the new word.

EXAMPLES: One model, then self-derivation plus a non-example; far-transfer: "આ પાત્ર જેવું વર્તન આજે ક્યાં જોવા મળે?"

CULTURAL ANCHORING: Anchor examples in Gujarat's own world per reference/gujarat_cultural_anchors.md; this student can handle the bank's less common anchors (અજરખના છાપ-બીબાની થપ-થપ, કચ્છી ભરતના ટાંકે ટાંકે ચમકતાં આભલાં, સાપુતારા-ડાંગના ઢોળાવે ઊતરતાં વાદળ) so long as they need no glossary and stay inside the child's own experience. The anchor illustrates the feeling only — never a fact about the text, the poet, or the chapter.

QUESTIONING: Start at interpretation: motive with evidence, alternative endings WRITTEN not just chosen, moral in own words plus a counter-case ("કોઈ એવો પ્રસંગ વિચારો જ્યાં આ બોધ લાગુ ન પડે"). (personification noticed, not named). હાઈકુ: student composes one (5-7-5) about a chapter image.

GRAMMAR: Student derives the rule, states it, names it, and stress-tests it: "આ નિયમ ક્યાં તૂટે છે?" Introduce the exception only after they hunt for it.

METACOGNITION: Full strategy talk: the student explains their method ("તમે આ જવાબ કેવી રીતે શોધ્યો?"), keeps an error log across sessions, self-edits with a rubric before you see the draft.

FEEDBACK: Self-correction first; category name second (કાળ / અનુગ / શબ્દક્રમ / જોડણી); recast into higher register and invite imitation.

PACING & ANSWERS: Brisk; long independent tasks (a short નિબંધ paragraph, a scene rewrite) with rubric; 2-attempt rule stands but attempts should be substantial.
```

## 5. Pipeline wiring

**Where the tier enters.** The orchestrator accepts an **optional** run parameter `tier ∈ {સહાય, ધોરણ, પ્રગત}`. **Omitted means ધોરણ.** The tier is a run parameter and a provenance note in the run log — it is **not** a plan field. No key of the 32 root keys or the 31 topic keys carries it, and none may be added to carry it; a plan that grows a `tier` key fails QC at Agent 13. If per-tier plans are wanted for the same chapter, they are separate runs into separate output directories, both keying the same provisional `chapter_id` form `gseb_eng_gujarati8_ch{N}` — **provisional, and the medium slot is the pack's single most dangerous silent-failure item: `eng` is the medium of instruction, not the subject language, and VERIFY-1 must resolve the real GSEB board/medium segments against the live server before any upload.**

**Who reads this file.** Phase 6 authoring. Agent 12 (`agents/12_runtime_authoring.md`) and Agent 16 (`agents/16_publication_authoring.md`) receive §3's descriptor block and §4's prompt for the selected tier alongside their standing references (`reference/teaching_voice_gu.md`, `reference/global_content_rules.md`, `reference/no_hallucination_policy.md`). Agent 10 (`agents/10_exercise_solutions.md`) receives §3 for scaffold shape only — never for coverage, which is total at every tier.

**Re-injection is mandatory, per call.** Prompt-level difficulty control decays within roughly nine turns. Agent 12 re-injects the std-8 + tier descriptor block in **every** generation call — per topic, per field batch — not once at session start. Difficulty is pinned **per exercise block**, never per session. A run that injected the tier once and then authored twenty topics has drifted, and the drift is invisible in the JSON: it shows up only as a sentence-length and gloss-density creep back toward ધોરણ.

**Which authored fields tighten per tier.**

| Field | સહાય | ધોરણ | પ્રગત |
|---|---|---|---|
| `explanation` (band 55–90 words, unchanged) | author at the **low end, 55–65**; sentences ≤ 10 words; every low-frequency word glossed inline with an English equivalent | mid-band; two-clause sentences and reported speech allowed | may sit at the high end; one notch denser register |
| `real_life_example` (55–90, exactly one anchor) | anchor drawn from the curated Gujarat examples bank only (મેળો, ઉત્તરાયણ, રસોડું, શેરી ક્રિકેટ); fully concrete | one anchor, familiar but not necessarily banked | anchor may be less familiar; may close on a far-transfer question |
| `key_terms` (3–6, `"શબ્દ — અર્થ"`) | top of the band, 5–6, including everyday words the text assumes | 4–5, academic and તળપદા words | low end, 3–4; rare and તત્સમ words as stretch |
| `concept_bullets` / `important_points` (3–4, keyword-first) | shortest phrasing; one idea per line | standard | may carry the abstraction first |
| `recall_questions` (2–3 per topic, Bloom-laddered, real model answers) | Bloom mix weighted `remember → understand`, **with at least one analyze-or-above item reached through a decomposed chain or a given chart in the prompt** — the Bloom ceiling never drops | `understand → apply/analyze` | `analyze → evaluate/create` |
| `difficult_words[].example` (module-level, fresh sentence) | shortest, most everyday sentence | standard | may use a richer construction |
| `brief_summary` / `summary` / `detailed_summary` | wording simplifies; **the 1 / 2–3 / 4–6 sentence bands and the strictly-increasing rule do not move** | | |

**What never varies by tier.** The phase-2 contract in full: 32 root keys, 31 topic keys, the objectives registry and its character-for-character inline mirror, the concept layer, the id grammar (chapter-continuous `.C{c}`, segment recalls as `.RQ{n}`, `MEDIA_ID_RE`), the closed emitted `topic_type` enum, the ordering constraint. Verbatim rules in full: `original_chunk` is the exact printed Gujarati, nothing dropped, paraphrased, transliterated or "corrected" — the medieval quoted lines of chs 3 and 6, the તળપદા of chs 7, 13 and 15, and the refrain-ellipsis conventions of chs 1 and 7 are identical bytes in a સહાય plan and a પ્રગત plan. Word-count bands, the exercise-coverage rule (every printed block answered, mapped, and never cut as a topic), the one-image-per-reading-scene and ≤ 1 `2d_tool` limits, and the no-fabrication policy are tier-invariant. `figures_of_speech` stays `[]` at std 8 unless the printed chapter itself names a device — and at no tier does it drive a question, because std 8 does not label craft.

Tier-keyed *support* (sentence frames, picture support, audio-assisted repeated reading) lives in authored prose and exercise scaffolds. It never becomes an extra media node: the media budget is set by reading scenes, not by tier.

**Routing when a tier goes wrong.** Explanation drifted out of the tier's sentence cap, gloss density collapsed, or a સહાય variant lost its higher-order recall item → **A12**, re-run with the descriptor block re-injected per call. Exercise scaffolds thinned coverage → **A10**. A missing or paraphrased `original_chunk` → **A5** — never a tier question. Any tier-driven change to ids, keys, or bands is a contract bug, owned by **A13**.
